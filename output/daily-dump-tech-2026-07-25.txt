It is July 25, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

AMD confirmed its upcoming processor roadmap during the company's Advancing AI event. Following the release of the EPYC 9006 Venice processors, which utilize the existing SP5 socket infrastructure and support twelve-channel DDR5 memory, the company committed to the Zen 7 Florence architecture. This new architecture features next-generation memory bandwidth improvements and Advanced Compute Engine capabilities designed to increase floating-point throughput by twenty-five percent over current EPYC generations. Zen 8 Ravenna is currently in active development, focusing on optical interconnects to reduce latency between core complexes by an additional fifteen percent compared to the Zen 7 design. These chips are intended for high-density data center environments, specifically targeting large-scale language model training workloads that require massive parallel processing power.

On the hardware pricing front, Qualcomm has notified customers of an impending price increase for its mobile and automotive chips. Starting September first, the company will raise prices by a double-digit percentage, citing an exhaustion of its ability to absorb rising manufacturing costs at the three-nanometer and two-nanometer process nodes. This adjustment follows a period where Qualcomm maintained steady pricing despite a fifteen percent increase in wafer costs from its primary foundry partners. The increase affects the entire Snapdragon product line, including the flagship series used in premium mobile handsets and the cockpit platforms integrated into electric vehicles.

Roku has also raised prices across its entire streaming hardware lineup. The entry-level high-definition streaming stick now costs thirty-nine dollars and ninety-nine cents, representing a ten-dollar increase from its previous retail price of twenty-nine dollars and ninety-nine cents. Other devices in the catalog, including the 4K-capable boxes and soundbar integrated systems, have seen price hikes ranging from twenty to fifty dollars. These adjustments bring the MSRP of the top-tier Ultra model to one hundred and forty-nine dollars, marking the first broad-based pricing realignment for the company since 2022.

In artificial intelligence, OpenAI has launched a new physical AI keypad peripheral. The device is designed to interface directly with the company's latest models via a dedicated low-latency wireless protocol, providing dedicated tactile inputs for model prompting and task execution. The hardware includes a haptic feedback surface and a dedicated key for triggering voice-to-text input, bypassing the need for traditional mouse and keyboard navigation during multi-step model interactions. This marks the company's first foray into proprietary input hardware, diverging from its previous focus on cloud-based software interfaces.

Also in the startup sector, the new lab Prentis, co-founded by Reid Hoffman and Mark Pincus, is in talks to raise one hundred million dollars in a Series A funding round. The company is focusing on the automation of routine computer tasks, prioritizing agentic workflows that operate independently within browser and operating system environments. Unlike prior iterations of coding assistants that focus on syntax completion, the Prentis model aims to execute multi-step business processes, such as expense reporting and supply chain procurement, by navigating proprietary software interfaces directly.

Midjourney has acquired the personalized astrology app Co-Star. The move signals an expansion for the image generation company as it integrates Co-Star's interaction model and conversational personality features into its broader AI platform. Midjourney plans to leverage Co-Star's existing database of user behavioral patterns and natural language processing models to refine its own text-to-image prompting interface. This acquisition follows Midjourney’s internal data indicating that users prefer stylistic guidance when generating complex visual assets, a capability Co-Star has refined through its personality-driven user experience.

Finally, Waymo is reportedly considering ending its partnership with Uber. The current contract between the two companies for autonomous ride-hailing services, which allows Uber users to book Waymo vehicles in specific metropolitan markets, is set to expire in May of 2028. Waymo currently operates a fleet of over four thousand vehicles across six major cities, while Uber maintains its own competing autonomous vehicle initiatives through separate partnerships with other robotics firms. The expiration of the current deal would grant Waymo full control over its customer data and fleet routing logistics within the Uber platform.
