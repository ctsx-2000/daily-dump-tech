Defense manufacturer Hadrian has raised one point three seven billion dollars in a new funding round, bringing the company to an eight billion dollar valuation. The startup focuses on automating factories to mass produce precision components for defense vehicles, including submarines. The capital injection comes from a mix of venture capital firms and strategic defense investors. Hadrian currently operates facilities in Southern California that utilize proprietary software to manage autonomous machine tools. These systems are designed to replace traditional manual machining processes that have historically created production bottlenecks for the Department of Defense. The firm intends to use these funds to expand its footprint to three additional sites within the next eighteen months, specifically targeting high-volume production of turbine blades and propulsion system parts.

Anthropic is moving to design its own custom hardware to run its Claude artificial intelligence models. This move aims to increase compute efficiency while reducing the company's reliance on external providers like Nvidia. The initiative follows a trend of large language model developers shifting away from off-the-shelf graphics processing units to custom silicon tailored for transformer architecture. Current inference costs for models like Claude 3.5 account for over sixty percent of the company’s operating expenses. Custom chips allow for higher memory bandwidth and specialized arithmetic logic units optimized specifically for the weight parameters used in large-scale generative models.

Security researchers at Google have identified a campaign where hackers are targeting employees at large United States financial firms. The attackers are using direct phone calls as a vector to compromise systems and extort victims for sensitive data. This social engineering technique, often referred to as vishing, bypasses traditional network-level firewalls by leveraging human interaction to gain initial access. The attackers impersonate IT help desk staff to request multi-factor authentication codes or to gain remote access to workstations. Once inside the perimeter, the hackers deploy credential-harvesting scripts to exfiltrate proprietary financial records and client transaction logs.

In other security news, a China-linked spyware operation known as LightSpy has been detected across thirteen countries, including the United States. Researchers identified the operators after one individual placed a food delivery order using their real name and official office address. The spyware, which is capable of exfiltrating contact lists, location data, and browser history, was originally deployed through compromised websites that redirected mobile users to malicious download pages. The campaign has specifically targeted political dissidents and regional government officials. Forensic analysis indicates the malware uses a modular architecture that allows it to remain dormant until it receives specific command-and-control instructions.

The AI music platform Suno is implementing new technical measures to address large-scale spam and unauthorized content. The company plans to introduce watermarking technology for generated audio and will enforce new download limits to curb automated abuse of its platform. These changes follow reports of high-frequency bot accounts scraping audio files to train rival models without authorization. The new watermarking system embeds an inaudible digital signature directly into the waveform, which remains detectable even after file compression or analog re-recording. Furthermore, the platform is migrating its download portal to a gated system that requires verified account credentials to prevent unauthenticated scraping.

The Federal Communications Commission has voted to end the national broadcast ownership cap rule. The decision, led by Chair Brendan Carr and Commissioner Olivia Trusty, removes the longstanding federal restrictions on the amount of broadcast stations a single entity can own. Previously, a single owner was prohibited from reaching more than thirty-nine percent of the national television audience. The removal of this cap allows media conglomerates to consolidate regional and local station portfolios into larger national networks without federal intervention. The decision follows a three-year review process that examined the competitive impact of streaming services on traditional broadcast market shares.

Finally, Representative Ro Khanna has called for the introduction of a Data Center Bill of Rights. The proposal aims to establish a federal framework granting local communities the legal authority to oppose the construction of new data centers in their jurisdictions. The bill addresses the increasing strain on local electrical grids and the significant water consumption requirements of high-density computing facilities. If enacted, the legislation would require data center developers to provide environmental impact statements and secure local zoning approval before federal land-use permits are granted. The bill also mandates transparent reporting of electricity usage statistics for all facilities exceeding fifty megawatts of power capacity.
