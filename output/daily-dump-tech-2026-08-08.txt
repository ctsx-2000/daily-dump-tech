It is August 08, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

Google has finalized the lineup for its August twelfth Made by Google hardware event. The presentation will be hosted by Trevor Noah, with appearances by Alex Cooper and Stephen Curry as the company prepares to debut the Pixel eleven hardware suite. The event marks an earlier release cycle compared to previous years, shifting from the traditional October window to mid-August to align with a broader refresh of the company’s internal hardware ecosystem. The Pixel eleven series replaces the Pixel ten line, with reports indicating a transition to a fully custom-designed processor architecture manufactured on a three-nanometer process.

In infrastructure news, Cloudflare has launched Kitesurf, a cloud-hosted browser engineered specifically for AI agents. By running in the cloud, the tool reduces computing overhead compared to Chromium-based environments, allowing developers to execute automated browser tasks with higher efficiency. Kitesurf operates by stripping away the graphical interface and rendering engine overhead required by traditional desktop browsers, which typically consume three to four gigabytes of memory per instance. By shifting this processing to remote containers, the service enables thousands of concurrent agent sessions on a single server cluster, replacing local emulation tools like Selenium or Puppeteer that were previously constrained by local hardware bandwidth and latency.

Rippling has released a new product called the AI Spend Console to manage corporate software expenses. The platform tracks individual and departmental AI usage and costs, serving as a centralized dashboard for companies to monitor return on investment regarding AI tool implementation. The console integrates directly with API logs from major large language model providers, automating the categorization of token usage across different business units. This replaces manual spreadsheet tracking and fragmented billing statements, providing granular visibility into consumption metrics for over two hundred distinct third-party generative AI services currently integrated into enterprise workflows.

Turning to browser policy, Microsoft Edge is ending support for the Manifest V two extensions platform. This shift will disable ad blockers that rely on the older framework, including uBlock Origin, aligning Edge with the current extension architecture enforced by Google Chrome. The transition mandates that all extensions migrate to the Manifest V three framework, which restricts the background processing capabilities and network request interceptors previously utilized by ad-blocking software to filter web content. This change affects approximately forty-two percent of the current Edge extension library, requiring developers to reconfigure their products to comply with the new declarative net request ruleset.

Apple has updated its trade-in program, increasing the residual value of several devices. Customers can now receive up to fourteen percent more for specific iPhone models, while Mac mini trade-in values have seen increases of up to twenty-eight percent. This adjustment brings the maximum trade-in credit for the iPhone fifteen Pro Max to eight hundred dollars, while the Mac mini M two Pro is now eligible for a credit ceiling of six hundred and fifty dollars. The update reflects a strategic adjustment to inventory acquisition cycles as the company prepares to incentivize upgrades ahead of its September hardware refresh.

Disney Plus is testing an AI-powered search feature for its streaming service. The tool allows users to input natural language queries or voice prompts to generate custom rows of recommended content based on their viewing history. The system utilizes a large language model to parse semantic intent from user prompts, replacing the static keyword-based search algorithm currently in place. The feature currently tests across twenty-five percent of the platform’s subscriber base, drawing from a metadata library of over ten thousand film and television titles.

Finally, the Government Accountability Office has released a report regarding the Department of Government Efficiency, or DOGE. Auditors found that ninety-six percent of the savings claims cited by the department regarding government grants are unverifiable, challenging the project's reported fiscal impact. The report examined seven hundred and fifty specific claims of cost reduction, noting that the department failed to provide the underlying financial documentation or baseline data necessary to calculate the reported savings. This audit follows the initial project launch in late 2025, which originally promised to reduce total federal discretionary spending by approximately five hundred billion dollars over a three-year period.
