OpenAI is expanding the capabilities of its free tier for ChatGPT users. Starting next week, free and Go plan subscribers will gain access to unlimited text generation, removing the previous caps on message volume. This change replaces the prior tiered system that restricted users to a set number of messages every three hours before reverting to a more limited, older model. The update grants free users access to the latest model architecture previously reserved for paid tiers, effectively doubling the available token window for non-paying accounts.

Moving to social media, X is winding down its long-standing revenue-sharing program for creators. The platform will launch a new Original Content Rewards system on September 8th, which limits eligibility to accounts with at least 500 verified followers. This replaces the previous payout model that distributed ad revenue based on impressions in the comments section of a creator's posts. Under the new guidelines, payouts are strictly calculated based on engagement metrics from original long-form video content uploaded directly to the platform, excluding reposts and image-based threads.

In enterprise software news, Rippling has released a tool called the AI Spend Console. The software is designed to track and report individual and team-level spending on artificial intelligence services, providing companies with data on their internal AI return on investment. The tool integrates directly with corporate expense management systems to categorize spending across more than forty distinct generative AI platforms. It provides automated reports on seat utilization and subscription inactivity, identifying instances where licenses for AI tools have remained unused for more than thirty consecutive days.

On the scientific front, Google DeepMind has released an open-source model called WeatherNext. The system is designed to generate accurate hurricane and storm predictions using lower-resolution atmospheric data than traditional numerical weather models. While traditional models require supercomputing clusters to process high-resolution atmospheric grids, WeatherNext uses deep learning to extrapolate global storm paths from datasets one-tenth the size of those used by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts. It achieves a 15 percent increase in the accuracy of landfall timing when measured against historical storm data from the past decade.

Expanding on global environmental monitoring, the Copernicus Browser has added new wildfire visualization features. This update allows for real-time tracking of fire spread and intensity, aimed at providing better data during the ongoing wildfire season. The system integrates high-resolution satellite imagery from the Sentinel-3 mission to calculate active fire radiative power every six hours. These data points are rendered into heat maps that indicate flame intensity in megawatts per kilometer, allowing emergency services to differentiate between controlled agricultural burns and uncontrolled forest fires.

In space exploration, NASA has confirmed that its Perseverance rover on Mars is now operating with high levels of autonomy. Roughly 90 percent of the total distance the vehicle has covered on the Martian surface has been navigated without manual input from mission control. This shift follows a recent firmware update to the rover’s navigation software, which reduced the time required to process terrain hazards by 40 percent. The rover now autonomously identifies and avoids rocks and steep slopes in real time, moving at a top speed of 120 meters per hour compared to the 20 meters per hour achieved during its initial deployment.

Finally, agricultural researchers have introduced a new breed of cattle specifically developed to graze in the shade of solar farms. These animals are purpose-bred to maintain vegetation around solar arrays, offering a solution for land management at large-scale renewable energy installations. The cattle exhibit a reduced flight response to the mechanical hum of inverters and demonstrate a physiological adaptation to lower light levels beneath ground-mounted solar panels. This breeding program addresses the maintenance costs of utility-scale solar sites, which previously required manual mowing of vegetation to prevent shading of photovoltaic cells.
