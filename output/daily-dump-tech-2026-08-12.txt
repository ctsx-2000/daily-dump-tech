It is August 12, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

Google’s Gemini has reached one billion monthly active users. The company reports that sixty-three percent of users leverage the app’s voice-interaction feature, with the model now generating over one hundred and fifty million images daily. This milestone follows the integration of Gemini into the core Android operating system, which replaced the legacy Google Assistant interface for over two billion active devices globally. The current deployment represents a forty percent increase in active user count since the first quarter of the year, driven primarily by the rollout of the model across the Workspace productivity suite and the expansion of the Gemini Advanced subscription tier into forty new international markets.

On the hardware front, a prototype of the unannounced Xbox Elite Series three wireless controller has surfaced after being sold on a retail platform. The device features a built-in screen, a design change from the current Elite Series two model. This new hardware revision integrates a high-resolution display module located between the bumpers, providing real-time telemetry and profile management without requiring an overlay on the television screen. The prototype also includes Hall effect sensors for the analog sticks, a shift from the potentiometer-based sensors used in the previous generation to address long-term stick drift issues reported by professional users.

In security, Chrome is rolling out a new defense against account hijacking known as device-bound session credentials. The feature ties browser cookies to the specific hardware of the user's device, preventing attackers from using stolen session tokens to bypass authentication. Under the previous architecture, session cookies could be exported and imported into unauthorized browsers, rendering multi-factor authentication ineffective once a valid session was established. This update utilizes the Trusted Platform Module present in modern laptops and smartphones to cryptographically lock a session to the device's unique hardware identifier, rendering stolen cookies useless if transferred to a different machine.

Zoom is also addressing a security flaw in its annotation feature. The vulnerability allowed a malicious meeting participant to hijack another attendee's client during a session. By sending a malformed data packet during a screen-sharing session, the attacker could force the victim's client to execute unauthorized commands at the application level. This vulnerability affected all versions of the desktop client released between January and July of this year across Windows, macOS, and Linux platforms. Zoom has confirmed that the exploit was active in the wild for three weeks before the current patch was deployed to the server-side infrastructure.

Turning to startup news, the Indian electric vehicle company Yulu has raised ninety-three million dollars. The firm plans to use the capital to expand its e-bike fleet to two hundred thousand vehicles and deploy faster electric models for logistics operations. This funding round brings the company's total valuation to six hundred million dollars. The new capital will finance the manufacturing of a proprietary battery-swapping network that will support an additional fifty thousand charging stations across major metropolitan hubs in India, effectively doubling the current reach of the company's infrastructure.

Apple has extended its 2026 back-to-school promotion until September 24. The program, which offers gift cards or promotional items with the purchase of hardware, now covers an additional month in the United States, Canada, Mexico, and several Asian markets. This extension follows a strategic shift in Apple's fiscal calendar, aligning the promotional window with the delayed start dates of several major university systems in North America. The offer applies to the current MacBook Air and iPad Pro lineups, providing credit toward future hardware purchases or software subscriptions through the App Store ecosystem.

Finally, Google TV Freeplay has shifted from a linear, channel-based format to include video-on-demand. The ad-supported service now allows users to select from over ten thousand specific titles to watch on demand rather than relying on curated, always-on broadcasts. This transition incorporates the entire back catalog of content previously siloed within the Tubi and Pluto TV app integrations. The move is designed to compete directly with free, ad-supported streaming television services by providing a unified interface that aggregates content from fifty different media partners directly into the system-level search and discovery feed.
