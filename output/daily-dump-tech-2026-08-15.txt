It is August 15, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

A critical security vulnerability in the macOS screen sharing feature allows remote attackers to gain unauthorized access to a system without a password. The flaw resides in the authentication handshake process, bypassing the Secure Enclave validation required for remote desktop sessions. Apple confirmed that threat actors are actively exploiting this vulnerability in the wild to intercept clipboard data and execute unauthorized remote commands. The bug affects all macOS Sequoia and Sonoma builds prior to the current emergency security update.

OpenAI is updating its ChatGPT application for Mac to include an opt-in Computer History feature. This update replaces the previous Chronicle capability and enables the model to store and recall how a user interacts with their operating system and desktop applications. The system logs frame-by-frame screen captures and cursor coordinates at five-second intervals, storing this metadata in a local encrypted database to provide context for long-term user queries. This shift represents a transition from ephemeral session memory to a persistent, searchable index of cross-application workflows.

In AI model releases, Meta has launched Glimmer, an open-weight model that can be downloaded and executed on local hardware. The release marks a departure from Meta’s more restricted Muse Spark model, which remains accessible only through company-controlled application programming interfaces. Glimmer utilizes a parameter count of 45 billion, enabling it to run on consumer-grade workstations equipped with at least 48 gigabytes of video memory. This architecture removes the reliance on cloud-based inference, allowing operators to process proprietary datasets entirely offline.

Google is updating its AI suite to allow users to remove visible watermarks from media generated by its tools. A new media watermark setting in Gemini and the video generator Flow now provides a toggle to disable the sparkle icons that previously appeared on AI-created images, videos, and music. This change affects the SynthID layer, which historically embedded a permanent, non-removable visual identifier in every output. While the visible icon is now optional, the underlying steganographic watermark remains present to satisfy international AI transparency standards.

On the regulatory front, the California Department of Motor Vehicles has granted permits to Aurora Innovation and Kodiak AI to begin testing self-driving trucks on state highways. These permits allow for autonomous operation on public roads under specific testing conditions, including a requirement for a human safety operator to remain in the driver’s seat during all transit. The state authorization covers 1,200 miles of highway infrastructure, focusing on high-traffic freight corridors between the San Francisco Bay Area and the Los Angeles basin.

New York City lawmakers are pushing for legislation to ban biometric surveillance at public venues like Madison Square Garden. The proposed ban targets the use of facial recognition scanners at entry points, following concerns from privacy advocates and local officials regarding how venue operators deploy this data. The bill explicitly prohibits the cross-referencing of ticket holder facial geometry against internal watchlists or third-party law enforcement databases. If passed, the regulation would require venues to delete all existing biometric templates within 90 days of the law taking effect.

Finally, the Unitree G1 robot, a four-foot-tall unit developed in China, is seeing increased attention as an affordable humanoid platform. The robot features 23 degrees of freedom and a weight of 35 kilograms, significantly lighter than the 80-kilogram industrial standards seen in prior generations. The unit is priced at 16,000 dollars, a reduction of roughly 70 percent compared to contemporary collaborative robots. While the hardware has gained prominence through social media demonstrations, its practical utility in commercial or industrial settings remains in the early stages of evaluation, specifically regarding battery life, which currently averages 90 minutes under full-load operation.
