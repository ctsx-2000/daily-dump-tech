It is August 18, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

Anthropic reports its annualized revenue has reached sixty-five billion dollars. The company added eighteen billion dollars in annualized revenue over the last two months, driven primarily by enterprise adoption of its latest large language models. This growth trajectory reflects a forty percent increase in subscription volume among Fortune 500 clients since the second quarter. Anthropic now processes over three billion tokens per second across its global data center infrastructure to meet this surge in demand.

Reddit is testing new audio and video experiences for its platform. The feature uses artificial intelligence to convert text-based posts and comments into spoken audio and video clips. This integration aims to mirror the engagement patterns seen on short-form video platforms by automatically generating visual overlays for long-form community discussions. The system pulls data directly from thread metadata to synthesize natural-sounding narration, targeting users who prefer passive consumption over traditional text scrolling.

YouTube will change how it calculates video view counts starting August 24. The platform will now count a view as soon as a video begins to play, aligning its metric with the standard used by TikTok and Instagram Reels. Previously, YouTube required a user to watch for at least thirty seconds to register a view, a threshold implemented in 2012 to prevent click-fraud. The update applies to both long-form uploads and Shorts, effectively unifying the measurement system across the entire YouTube ecosystem.

Apple released iOS 26.6.1 today. The software update addresses more than twenty security vulnerabilities across the iPhone operating system, including three high-severity kernel flaws that allowed for arbitrary code execution. These vulnerabilities impacted all devices running iOS 26.6 and earlier versions. The patch specifically mitigates memory corruption issues identified by third-party researchers during private audits conducted last month.

The Supreme Court has rejected a petition from Verizon seeking a forty-seven million dollar refund of a fine issued by the Federal Communications Commission. The fine relates to the carrier’s historical practices regarding the sale of device-location data to third-party aggregators between 2017 and 2019. The court’s refusal to hear the case leaves in place a lower court ruling that the Federal Communications Commission acted within its regulatory authority to penalize carriers for failing to protect customer privacy.

In startup news, Higgsfield has raised four hundred million dollars in a Series B funding round. The AI video and image generation company is now valued at five point four billion dollars, a fourfold increase over its valuation eight months ago. The capital injection is earmarked for the expansion of its proprietary cloud rendering farm, which currently supports real-time generative video output for mobile applications. Higgsfield’s platform now hosts over twelve million active monthly users who utilize its tools to generate synthetic media assets for social advertising.

Finally, a team of former SpaceX engineers is developing a robotic factory designed for the automated production of steel parts. The project focuses on industrial manufacturing processes rather than full autonomy, utilizing modular robotic arms to perform precision welding and material shaping. The factory replaces traditional manual assembly lines with a closed-loop computer-aided manufacturing system that reduces material waste by thirty percent. The facility is expected to reach an output capacity of five hundred units per hour by the end of the next fiscal quarter.
