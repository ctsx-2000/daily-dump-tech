LG Display has unveiled a new panel technology branded as Flip OLED. The company reports the technology increases aperture ratio by fifty-five percent, resulting in up to one point six times higher brightness and two point four times longer lifespan than previous OLED iterations. The process is designed for panels ranging from one to one hundred inches. The Flip OLED architecture utilizes a tandem structure that stacks two organic light-emitting layers, effectively reducing the current density required to achieve specific luminance levels. This modification replaces the single-stack design found in standard smartphone and television panels, aiming to mitigate burn-in risks that have historically affected high-brightness organic diode displays. By shifting the pixel driving circuitry behind the light-emitting layer, the design maximizes the light-transmitting surface area per pixel.

In AI software, Meta has released a dedicated desktop application for Meta AI on macOS. The move signals an effort to integrate its generative models directly into user workflows outside of the web browser. The native application provides a persistent side-panel interface that remains accessible while other software is in use, leveraging the macOS windowing system to allow for drag-and-drop file analysis. This release marks a shift from the web-only model used for the Llama 3 series, allowing the application to utilize system-level permissions for screen capture and localized file processing. The client is built using a custom framework that bypasses browser-based memory limitations, providing a dedicated local cache for conversation history.

Waymo is expanding its next-generation robotaxi service, branded as the Ojai, to all riders in three additional cities. The company states this vehicle platform is central to its current strategy to achieve mass-scale operations and improve unit economics. The Ojai platform features a sensor suite with thirty-two cameras and six long-range lidar units, replacing the previous sensor array that required distinct housing structures on the vehicle roof. By integrating these sensors directly into the chassis during the manufacturing process, Waymo reduces the per-vehicle production cost by approximately forty percent compared to retrofitted models. The expansion covers a combined service area of two hundred square miles across three major metropolitan transit zones.

On the venture front, the chief executive of AI coding startup Cognition has denied reports that SpaceX attempted to acquire the company. SpaceX has recently acquired the coding assistant platform Cursor to scale its enterprise AI capabilities. The acquisition of Cursor involves the integration of its proprietary large language model fine-tuning processes into SpaceX’s internal software development pipeline. This move is designed to automate the generation of ground-control software and telemetry analysis tools. While Cognition remains an independent entity, the acquisition of Cursor represents a consolidation of coding-agent startups currently valued at over two billion dollars in total private equity.

Hardware manufacturer Framework has issued a response to reports of bricked mainboards following a BIOS update for its Ryzen seven-zero-forty series laptops. The company is currently replacing affected mainboards, including those outside of the standard warranty period. The failure occurred during the firmware write sequence, which triggered an irreversible boot-loop state in the security processor. Framework is shipping replacement boards equipped with a revised recovery header that allows for external firmware reflashing if the primary SPI flash memory becomes corrupted. This policy applies to all units sold within the last twenty-four months, regardless of the original purchase date or ownership status.

NASA has officially called off its mission to rescue the Swift gamma-ray observatory. Without a viable path to repair or boost its orbit, the agency expects the satellite to reenter the atmosphere later this year. Launched in 2004, the observatory has experienced a degradation in its reaction wheel performance, which is essential for maintaining the precise pointing required to detect transient high-energy events. The satellite currently resides at an altitude of four hundred kilometers and is losing approximately two kilometers of altitude per month due to atmospheric drag. NASA has ceased all science operations to preserve remaining battery power for a controlled final data dump before the satellite disintegrates.

Finally, the Federal Communications Commission has abolished its previous one-gigabit-per-second speed goal for broadband. The agency now mandates that speed standards remain technologically neutral, citing that previous metrics were unfair to slower connection technologies. This decision removes the requirement for municipalities to reach specific throughput benchmarks to qualify for federal infrastructure grants. The new framework allows for a multi-tiered definition of high-speed access based on regional latency and packet loss metrics rather than raw download speeds. This change impacts over four thousand local service providers that previously relied on the gigabit standard to secure federal funding allocations.
