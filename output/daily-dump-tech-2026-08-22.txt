LinkedIn says over one million users have clicked its new AI slop button since the feature launched in late July. The tool, located in the post options menu, allows users to report content they identify as low-quality AI-generated material to the platform. This reporting mechanism replaces the previous general feedback form, which required users to manually categorize content types rather than specifically flagging algorithmic noise. The data collected from these reports is fed directly into a new moderation filter designed to reduce the visibility of posts that share structural similarities with common generative AI outputs. Currently, the reporting feature is active for all accounts in North America and Europe, with a global expansion scheduled for the end of the third quarter.

In the automotive sector, Tesla is issuing a recall affecting three million vehicles in China due to safety concerns regarding door handles. The recall covers Model Three and Model Y vehicles produced between January 2021 and July 2026. The issue involves doors that may fail to unlock during a crash, with regulators requiring both physical warning stickers and camera system updates to resolve the defect. This update replaces the existing emergency release protocol, which previously required manual mechanical intervention if the electronic system failed. The company has stated that the software component of the fix will be deployed via an over-the-air update, while the physical hardware components will be installed at service centers at no cost to the owners.

Google is rolling out a new feature for publishers to mitigate traffic losses from AI-driven search results. The company is introducing a preference setting that allows readers to designate specific sites as preferred sources, which will prioritize that publisher's content across Search, Discover, and Google News. This feature replaces the previous system of broad site-wide ranking signals, instead giving individual users granular control over their information stream. The setting is synchronized across all devices signed into a primary Google account, ensuring that publisher preference is maintained whether the user is on mobile or desktop platforms. This change follows reports of a twelve percent decline in organic referral traffic for niche news outlets since the integration of AI summaries earlier this year.

In artificial intelligence research, testing has revealed that Anthropic’s Claude Opus four-point-six model can be prompted to generate sexually explicit content. While the company maintains a policy against the generation of such material, researchers found that the restriction can be bypassed with specific input patterns. This exploit targets the model's safety training by utilizing multi-layered persona simulation, which effectively masks forbidden intent behind benign narrative structures. The research team demonstrated that the model's current guardrails, which were updated last month, failed to identify these conversational patterns in eighty-five percent of controlled test cases. Anthropic has acknowledged the findings and confirmed that a model recalibration is currently undergoing final validation testing.

On the business front, the accounting automation startup Rillet has achieved unicorn status after raising one hundred million dollars. The funding round was completed within forty-eight hours following a board meeting where the company presented its current growth metrics to investors including Sequoia and Iconiq. This capital injection brings the company's total valuation to one point two billion dollars, a significant increase from its six hundred million dollar valuation recorded during its series B round last year. Rillet intends to use the funds to scale its engineering headcount by forty percent and expand its infrastructure to support mid-market enterprise clients in the financial services sector. The company currently reports a recurring revenue growth rate of two hundred percent year-over-year.

Walmart is finally adding support for tap-to-pay services at its retail locations. Starting August 24, Apple Pay and Google Pay will be available at select Walmart and Sam's Club stores, with a full rollout across all United States locations scheduled for completion by the end of this year. This change replaces the company's proprietary Walmart Pay system as the primary focus for contactless transactions, though the legacy app will remain active for loyalty rewards and receipt tracking. The transition requires the replacement of existing point-of-sale terminals at over four thousand seven hundred stores nationwide. This move marks the end of an eight-year period during which the retailer restricted contactless payments to its own mobile application to avoid third-party transaction fees.

Finally, a new lawsuit alleges that Zillow has contributed to rent inflation in New York City by creating a supply shock through hidden listings. The plaintiffs claim the platform utilized undisclosed inventory data to manipulate market perception and artificially raise rental prices for consumers. The lawsuit argues that by delaying the publication of available units by forty-eight hours, the platform created a false sense of scarcity that encouraged landlords to increase asking prices by an average of five percent. The filing cites internal documents suggesting that these delays were programmed into the platform's feed to maximize engagement on premium listings. Zillow has stated that its listing delays are a result of automated verification protocols intended to prevent fraudulent entries, a process it claims is standard across the industry.
