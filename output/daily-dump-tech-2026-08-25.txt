It is August 25. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

Zillow and Redfin have reached a settlement with the Federal Trade Commission regarding their rental listings partnership. The deal concludes an investigation into allegations that Zillow paid Redfin to syndicate listings in exchange for Redfin ending separate advertising contracts, a practice the commission argued stifled competition. Under the terms of the agreement, both companies are prohibited from entering into agreements that restrict competition for third-party rental listing services for a period of ten years. The settlement specifically addresses the 2021 syndication agreement that required Redfin to prioritize Zillow’s platform while preventing Redfin from integrating with other competing listing aggregators. This resolution follows a two-year inquiry into how these agreements impacted the rental market for property management companies and individual landlords, who previously utilized multiple platforms to reach prospective tenants.

Apple is reversing a planned change to its iCloud Plus Hide My Email feature. The company confirmed it will continue using the icloud dot com domain for masked email addresses rather than migrating them to a new, unified domain as it had previously announced in June. The original plan would have forced all users to transition to a new domain structure, potentially disrupting existing email forwarding rules and automated filters for users who rely on the service for privacy. By maintaining the current domain, Apple preserves the existing infrastructure that manages millions of unique masked addresses created since the feature was integrated into iCloud Plus in 2021. The reversal ensures that third-party services which whitelist specific sender domains will not require updates to their security protocols to accommodate the transition.

On the regulatory front, the Alabama attorney general has launched an investigation into OpenAI following a cybersecurity incident. The probe centers on the company's disclosure that one of its internal models was compromised, leading to unauthorized access to datasets managed by Hugging Face. The Alabama investigation focuses on whether the breach involved the exposure of sensitive consumer information belonging to state residents and whether OpenAI met its statutory obligations regarding incident notification timelines. This inquiry adds to existing oversight by the Federal Trade Commission and various European data protection authorities, which are currently auditing how generative AI companies store, secure, and manage large-scale training datasets.

In the wearable tech sector, Oura is reportedly preparing for an initial public offering scheduled for September. The company is seeking a valuation exceeding sixteen billion dollars as it enters the public market. This valuation represents a significant increase from its last private funding round in 2022, when the company was valued at two point five five billion dollars. Oura currently commands approximately sixty percent of the smart ring market, competing against newer entrants from Samsung and Amazfit. The company has sold over three million units of its fitness-tracking rings to date, with a recurring revenue model driven by its monthly subscription service for advanced health analytics.

Google is rolling out a new feature for Android seventeen designed to mitigate motion sickness for passengers using devices in moving vehicles. The capability, called Motion Assist, displays visual cues on the screen to help align a user's sensory perception with the movement of the vehicle. By using the device’s internal gyroscope and accelerometer, the software generates subtle, dynamic overlays that shift in correspondence with the vehicle's acceleration, braking, and turns. This feature is intended to reduce the sensory conflict between the vestibular system and visual input, which clinical research identifies as the primary cause of motion sickness in high-speed transit.

In startup news, drone delivery company Airbound has raised thirty-seven million dollars in a funding round led by Greenoaks, with participation from DoorDash and investor Lachy Groom. The company is developing lightweight drones intended to compete directly with traditional ground-based delivery logistics. Airbound’s hardware, known as the Tracer, is designed to weigh less than five kilograms while maintaining the ability to carry a one-kilogram payload over distances of up to twenty kilometers. The capital injection is earmarked for scaling manufacturing capacity and expanding flight testing operations to meet the requirements for beyond-visual-line-of-sight flight permits in urban environments.

General Motors is facing federal scrutiny over its electronic brake systems. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration is investigating the company's eBoost brake-by-wire technology following hundreds of reports, including at least twenty-two confirmed crashes. The investigation spans approximately three million vehicles produced between 2020 and 2024, including popular SUV and truck models across the Chevrolet, GMC, and Cadillac brands. The agency is looking for evidence of a common software or hardware failure point that causes a sudden loss of braking power, which drivers have reported as occurring without warning while the vehicle is in motion.

Finally, the rapid expansion of data centers is driving a significant increase in proposed natural gas power projects across the United States. Energy sector data shows that developers are increasingly turning to new gas generation to meet the high-capacity, reliable power demands required by large-scale computing infrastructure. Current projections from the Department of Energy indicate that data center power consumption is expected to double by 2030, necessitating the addition of approximately thirty-five gigawatts of new generation capacity. To sustain the continuous, twenty-four-seven cooling and processing requirements of AI server clusters, developers are prioritizing natural gas over renewable sources, which currently lack the storage density and base-load consistency required by modern hyperscale facilities.
