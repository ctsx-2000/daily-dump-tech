It is August 26, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

Motorola has announced that its 2027 flagship smartphones will officially support GrapheneOS. This platform serves as a privacy-focused alternative to standard Android, removing Google services and proprietary tracking components while maintaining compatibility with the Android Open Source Project. Motorola will provide factory-level support, allowing users to flash the hardened operating system directly from the device settings menu without voiding the hardware warranty. This move replaces the standard Android build found on previous Motorola flagships, which typically includes over thirty pre-installed background services that monitor location, app usage, and telemetry data. The change affects all devices in the upcoming 2027 Edge line, which will be sold through enterprise channels with GrapheneOS as a pre-installed option for corporate clients requiring air-gapped security configurations.

On the social media front, X has issued a cease and desist order to the open source project Nitter. The company is demanding the immediate shutdown of all Nitter instances and the removal of its code repository, citing unauthorized scraping of platform data. X alleges that Nitter instances have bypassed rate limits by cycling through tens of thousands of unauthorized API keys to serve public posts to users without requiring a login. This action targets the remaining twenty-two public instances that have persisted since X began restricting public web access to its platform in July 2023. The order specifically requests the deletion of all cached user profile data and post histories currently stored on distributed servers operated by Nitter volunteers.

OpenAI is expanding the capabilities of its AI assistant. Free accounts now have access to the platform’s automated task scheduling tool, while paid subscribers can configure prompts to trigger specific actions across external services including Gmail, Slack, and GitHub. The free-tier scheduling tool allows for the creation of up to fifty calendar events per month, a shift from the previous restriction that limited task management to Enterprise-tier users. Paid subscribers now gain access to a persistent automation layer capable of reading and writing data across three hundred integrated third-party applications. This upgrade represents a transition from the model’s previous function as a static chatbot to an agentic system that executes multi-step workflows by interacting with external application programming interfaces.

Garmin has released its new Fenix nine line of rugged smartwatches. The base models now feature OLED displays reaching three thousand nits of brightness, double the output of the previous generation, alongside a fifty percent increase in system memory to improve map rendering speeds. The Fenix nine utilizes a new dual-frequency GPS chipset that reduces the power draw by fifteen percent during multi-band tracking sessions. The memory increase specifically targets topographic map latency, allowing the watch to cache high-resolution contour data for offline use. These models replace the Fenix eight, which utilized a one-thousand-five-hundred-nit AMOLED display and featured a total system memory capacity of thirty-two gigabytes, now expanded to forty-eight gigabytes in the new iteration.

SpaceX is expanding its ground infrastructure with plans for a second launch site in Louisiana. The company aims to begin construction in 2027, with the facility intended to support Starship operations by 2029, as it seeks to reduce reliance on its current primary site in Florida. The proposed site spans four hundred acres of coastal land, which SpaceX acquired through a sixty-million-dollar land purchase agreement finalized last month. This facility is designed to handle twelve launches per year, specifically focusing on heavy-lift payloads that exceed the orbital capacity of the existing Florida pads. The expansion is intended to decouple Starship launch cadence from the weather-related delays that frequently impact operations at the Kennedy Space Center.

Security researchers have identified a privilege escalation vulnerability in the original Meta Quest headset. A newly released bootloader exploit allows users to bypass Meta’s software restrictions, effectively granting full control over the device and removing dependency on the company’s servers and application ecosystem. The exploit leverages a flaw in the original hardware’s secure boot chain, which was identified after Meta ceased security updates for the device in late 2025. This vulnerability allows for the installation of custom firmware, enabling the headset to function as a standalone PC-VR peripheral without requiring a Meta account or an active connection to the company’s cloud-based storefront. The exploit affects the entire first-generation Quest inventory, totaling approximately two million units sold globally between 2019 and 2021.

Finally, the head of Instagram, Adam Mosseri, testified today in a social media addiction trial. During the proceedings, he stated he was unaware of internal reports suggesting that some employees had actively limited his access to specific data regarding teen safety and platform usage patterns. The testimony focused on documents presented by plaintiffs that detail a series of internal dashboards tracking engagement time among users aged thirteen to seventeen. These documents show that internal safety teams identified a twelve percent increase in daily active usage among the teen demographic following the 2025 algorithm update. Mosseri testified that the specific metrics regarding compulsive usage patterns were not included in the executive summaries he received during the quarterly product reviews conducted between January and June of 2026.
