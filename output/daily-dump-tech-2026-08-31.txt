It is August 31, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

An archive spanning over 12 terabytes of Steam depot data covering the years 2003 to 2013 has leaked online. The dump contains over 70,000 unique build manifests, providing access to pre-release binaries and internal source assets for thousands of titles previously unavailable to the public. This collection includes early development files for Half-Life 2 Episode 3, which was officially canceled by Valve in 2006, and deleted level geometry and texture files from the original 2007 Portal release. The data originated from a misconfigured server previously used by third-party testing contractors.

Nvidia is committing 3.5 billion dollars to Taiwanese chipmaker MediaTek. This capital injection follows a series of joint ventures between the two companies focused on integrating Nvidia graphics IP into MediaTek’s Dimensity mobile chipsets. The investment solidifies Nvidia’s supply chain access as major cloud providers and hyperscalers increasingly transition from general-purpose GPUs to custom silicon designs tailored for specific large language model training workloads. This move counters the ongoing trend where firms like Google and Amazon shift their internal infrastructure spending away from merchant silicon toward proprietary hardware.

On the semiconductor manufacturing front, Chinese firm CXMT is scheduled to begin mass production of LPDDR6 RAM this September. This release moves the company into a higher bandwidth category, following their previous focus on LPDDR4X and DDR5 modules. Industry reports indicate that total output will be constrained by current lithography equipment limitations, specifically regarding the availability of high-numerical aperture extreme ultraviolet machines required for node shrinking. This production milestone arrives as demand for high-speed volatile memory rises in the mobile sector to accommodate on-device generative AI applications.

OpenAI is facing stricter regulatory oversight in the European Union. ChatGPT has been officially designated as a Very Large Online Search Engine under the EU Digital Services Act. This classification applies to platforms with more than 45 million monthly active users in the union. The designation requires the company to implement mandatory risk mitigation measures regarding user mental health, the protection of minors, and the moderation of illegal content, alongside annual independent audits of its recommendation algorithms.

In social media updates, Instagram is adjusting its platform policy to address AI-generated accounts. The company is rebranding its existing content label to an AI-generated profile tag. This badge must be displayed on any account where the primary persona is synthetic. This policy update replaces the previous, broader disclosure requirements that allowed for ambiguous labeling. The platform now requires automated systems to detect and verify these accounts, ensuring users can distinguish between human-operated entities and autonomous agents.

The VLC media player has officially surpassed 7 billion cumulative downloads. Despite the dominance of subscription-based streaming services that rely on proprietary web-based players, the project remains a primary utility for local media playback across Windows, macOS, and Linux. The software maintained its growth trajectory by expanding support for high-efficiency video coding and AV1 codecs, which have become industry standards for high-resolution 8K streaming.

Finally, the prediction market platform Kalshi has issued a lifetime ban to former United States Representative George Santos. The company also fined Santos over 71,000 dollars, alleging that he attempted to manipulate market outcomes on the platform through a series of coordinated trades. The platform's internal compliance logs indicated that the account activity violated the terms of service regarding market integrity and the prohibition of wash trading.
