It is September first. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

John Ternus has officially begun his tenure as the Chief Executive Officer of Apple, marking the first leadership change at the company since twenty-eleven. Ternus, who previously served as the senior vice president of hardware engineering, oversaw the transition of the Mac lineup to custom silicon and the development of the Vision Pro headset. Former CEO Tim Cook is transitioning to the role of executive chairman on the board of directors, shifting away from day-to-day operations after overseeing a period where Apple’s market capitalization grew from three hundred fifty billion dollars to over three trillion dollars.

Apple has submitted evidence to investigators alleging that a former employee destroyed records related to the theft of proprietary data for OpenAI. The company claims this employee intentionally deleted files after learning he was under investigation for potential data misappropriation involving internal documentation regarding machine learning infrastructure. Legal filings indicate that forensic analysts recovered fragments of the deleted communications, which allegedly detail the transfer of sensitive intellectual property to personal storage devices shortly before the employee’s resignation.

On the legal front, the Federal Trade Commission and twenty-two states have filed a lawsuit against Amazon. The complaint alleges that the company implemented a secret surcharge scheme to increase advertising costs for businesses operating on its platform. Regulators claim these undisclosed fees effectively increased the cost of doing business for third-party sellers by an average of twelve percent while simultaneously inflating consumer prices for retail items. The suit asserts that Amazon used its dominant market position to force sellers into the internal ad ecosystem to maintain search visibility.

PlayStation is set to pay seven point eight five million dollars to resolve a class-action lawsuit. The settlement follows litigation regarding user eligibility and platform practices, specifically focusing on the company’s digital storefront policies and refund procedures. The agreement covers approximately one hundred fifty thousand account holders who purchased digital content that was subsequently removed from their libraries due to licensing expirations. Affected users are slated to receive automatic credits or cash disbursements ranging from twenty to fifty dollars depending on the volume of content lost.

In security, a twelve-terabyte archive from internal Valve servers has leaked online. The data includes development builds and assets from two thousand three to two thousand thirteen, featuring early versions of titles such as Portal two and Half-Life. This dump contains source code, unreleased level geometry, and internal developer communications documenting the transition from GoldSrc to the Source engine. Security researchers confirmed the archive contains over two million individual files, representing the most significant unauthorized release of internal company assets in the firm’s history.

Turning to hardware, NVIDIA’s DLSS five neural rendering is launching this week, but early reports indicate it is causing significant performance issues on older hardware. Users with RTX thirty-series graphics cards are seeing frame rates drop by ninety-seven percent when the feature is enabled, while the technology remains exclusive to RTX fifty-series support. Independent benchmarks show that the rendering pipeline utilizes a different instruction set architecture than previous iterations, leading to massive overhead for cards lacking dedicated tensor cores designed for the updated neural network.

Finally, Google has increased the price of its Google TV Streamer. Two years after its initial launch, the four-K streaming device now retails for one hundred forty-nine dollars, an increase of fifty dollars over its original price. This adjustment follows a hardware revision that incorporates a faster processor and increased RAM to support the updated Google TV interface. The previous iteration of the device will be phased out of the retail channel by the end of the current fiscal quarter.
