Anthropic has released two new models, Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1. These updates focus on reasoning capabilities and token efficiency for enterprise workflows, with updated system cards available on the company website. The Fable 5.1 model introduces a 20 percent reduction in latency for long-context retrieval tasks compared to the 5.0 version, while Mythos 5.1 increases the context window to two million tokens. These models replace the previous iterations across the company’s API endpoints, specifically targeting high-volume data analysis tasks that previously required manual chunking.

In startup news, AfterQuery has reached a 3.2 billion dollar valuation. The AI model-training company achieved this milestone five months after its initial Series A round, marking the fastest unicorn valuation in Y Combinator history. The company secured 800 million dollars in this latest funding cycle, led by venture firms that previously backed competitors in the synthetic data space. This valuation represents a 400 percent increase over the company’s December 2025 assessment, following their deployment of a proprietary data-cleaning pipeline now used by four of the top ten global large language model labs.

On the legal front, OpenAI is facing 30 new lawsuits related to the Tumbler Ridge shooting. Plaintiffs are escalating claims to include aiding and abetting, with the filings specifically naming executive Chris Lehane. These lawsuits allege that the company’s platform provided actionable tactical information that directly influenced the perpetrator’s methodology during the event. The complaints seek unspecified damages for negligence and failure to implement sufficient safety guardrails, citing internal documents that suggest the company was aware of potential misuse scenarios involving high-risk queries.

Turning to enterprise software, Microsoft Outlook experienced a widespread outage on Monday. Thousands of users reported connectivity issues throughout the day, though the company has not yet released a technical breakdown of the underlying cause. The disruption affected the Exchange Online platform, preventing users from accessing email services, calendar synchronization, and integrated task management tools for approximately seven hours. Global telemetry data shows the outage peaked at 10:00 AM Eastern Time, impacting users primarily across North American and European data centers before service was fully restored by 5:00 PM.

In the ride-sharing sector, Uber is laying off 3,300 employees, representing 10 percent of its global workforce. The company stated the reduction is intended to streamline management layers and refocus investment into its delivery and robotaxi divisions. This cut follows a restructuring of the company's middle management tier, which saw a 15 percent headcount reduction in regional operations oversight. These roles were primarily concentrated in the legacy passenger transport division, which has seen stagnant growth compared to the 22 percent year-over-year revenue increase in the Uber Eats and autonomous freight sectors.

Tesla has officially launched its robotaxi service using the steering-wheel-free Cybercab. Two years after the initial reveal, the vehicles are now operational, marking the company's transition from autonomous testing to a commercial fleet model. The service is currently active in three major metropolitan areas, utilizing a fleet of 1,500 vehicles that operate without human safety drivers. Each unit features the updated hardware suite, which Tesla claims provides a 30 percent improvement in obstacle detection range compared to the sensor array used in their previous model year passenger vehicles.

Finally, a major Border Gateway Protocol hijacking incident recently infected production networks due to a configuration error. The event caused widespread traffic misdirection and has prompted renewed discussion regarding network routing security and the fragility of current infrastructure protocols. The misconfiguration originated from a Tier 1 provider’s automated policy update, which incorrectly advertised 400,000 internal network prefixes to the global routing table. This resulted in a total traffic diversion for major financial and cloud service providers for approximately 45 minutes, rendering affected services unreachable until the incorrect route announcements were withdrawn and propagated globally.
