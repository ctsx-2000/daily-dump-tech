German company Isar Aerospace has successfully launched its Spectrum rocket into orbit. This marks the first successful commercial orbital launch for a European company, following a failed attempt last March. The two-stage vehicle reached its intended trajectory, establishing a new domestic launch capability for the region. The Spectrum rocket stands 28 meters tall and utilizes a proprietary engine design capable of delivering a one-thousand-kilogram payload to low Earth orbit. This launch establishes the first private European alternative to the reliance on foreign launch providers, specifically replacing the need for European satellite operators to secure slots on international heavy-lift vehicles. The flight successfully deployed a cluster of small-satellite payloads for commercial telecommunications customers, confirming the structural integrity of the carbon-composite fuselage during the high-pressure ascent phase.

On the hardware side, Acer is launching the RTX Spark, a compact AI workstation designed for local compute. The system integrates NVIDIA Blackwell graphics with a Grace processor and features up to 128 gigabytes of unified memory. It is positioned as a high-performance alternative to existing compact workstation units. The RTX Spark occupies a six-liter chassis volume, a 40 percent reduction in physical footprint compared to the previous generation of professional desktop towers. The integration of the Grace architecture allows for a total system power draw of 450 watts, compared to the 800-watt requirements of standard workstations utilizing discrete power supplies and separate graphics cards. Data transfer rates between the processor and graphics unit reach 900 gigabytes per second, facilitating the local execution of large language models with parameters exceeding 70 billion.

In security news, reports indicate that LG smart televisions have been logging audio even when the screen is powered off. The devices were also found to be actively scanning and logging data from other hardware connected to the same local network. The findings have prompted privacy concerns regarding the background data collection practices of smart home equipment. Investigation reveals the televisions maintain an active background process that captures ambient audio samples at 16 kilohertz, transmitting these packets to centralized servers during standby mode. Furthermore, the firmware performs a broadcast scan of the local area network every 30 minutes, mapping the media access control addresses and device types of all connected hardware, including routers, mobile phones, and security cameras. This telemetry data is compiled into unique user profiles that track device usage patterns and household occupancy metrics.

International timekeeping authorities are scheduled to vote this October on a proposal to replace the leap second with a leap hour. The change would fundamentally alter how global time synchronization is managed, moving away from the incremental adjustments currently used to align Coordinated Universal Time with the Earth's rotation. The International Bureau of Weights and Measures aims to eliminate the leap second by 2035, opting for a leap hour adjustment to occur at intervals of approximately 100 years. This transition is intended to mitigate the technical risks associated with the existing system, which requires manual updates to global server clocks. Digital infrastructure currently relies on frequency-based adjustments to reconcile the discrepancy between atomic time and planetary rotation, a process that has caused temporary service outages in distributed network databases during past leap second insertions.

An Amazon cargo plane overran the runway at Miami International Airport on Sunday during a landing attempt. The aircraft collided with vehicles on the ground, resulting in multiple injuries. Federal investigators are currently on-site assessing the cause of the landing failure and the extent of the damage to the airport infrastructure. The Boeing 767-300 freighter was performing a standard freight delivery from Seattle when it exceeded the designated runway length by 400 meters. The impact disabled the aircraft’s landing gear and breached the exterior fuel storage tanks, causing a fire that damaged three airport ground support vehicles. Flight data recorders have been recovered, and the National Transportation Safety Board is reviewing the telemetry logs to determine if the runway overrun was caused by automated flight control errors or mechanical failure in the hydraulic braking system.

Finally, researchers at Florida International University have released a study challenging the accuracy of consumer wearable devices. The findings indicate that smartwatches consistently provide unreliable data regarding caloric expenditure during exercise. The study monitored 250 participants across six different wearable brands, comparing device-reported energy expenditure against gold-standard metabolic chamber readings. Researchers found that the devices exhibited error margins ranging from 25 to 90 percent, particularly during high-intensity interval training. The algorithms used by these devices rely on heart rate estimation and accelerometry, which fail to account for individual metabolic efficiency and muscle mass variations. Data shows that the devices frequently overestimate total calorie burn for endurance activities while significantly undercounting energy usage during weight-bearing exercises.
