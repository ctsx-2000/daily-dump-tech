OpenAI has announced a solution to the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem. This mathematical milestone has remained unsolved for roughly ninety years and relates to the physics of fluid and gas flow. The company detailed its findings in a blog post on Tuesday. The Navier-Stokes equations serve as the foundation for modern aerodynamics, weather prediction, and hydraulic engineering. OpenAI’s approach utilizes a specialized neural architecture designed to prove the existence of smooth, globally defined solutions in three dimensions, a feat that has eluded mathematicians since the equations were formalized in the nineteenth century. The proof addresses the Millennium Prize Problem criteria established by the Clay Mathematics Institute, which previously carried a one million dollar award for the first valid, peer-reviewed demonstration.

In AI business news, Suno has replaced its underlying models with a new version trained entirely on licensed music. This shift follows multiple copyright lawsuits filed against the company, and the organization states the new model does not utilize data from previous training sets. The previous generation of Suno models faced litigation from major record labels, including Sony Music and Universal Music Group, regarding the alleged ingestion of copyrighted song files without authorization. The new model relies on a curated dataset of over ten million tracks secured through direct licensing agreements with rights holders. Suno reports that this architectural transition resulted in a twelve percent increase in audio fidelity and a reduction in artifacts during the generation of complex polyphonic sequences.

Security researchers are tracking a rise in the theft of Claude API tokens from subscriber accounts. Anthropic has issued warnings to users after reports emerged of accounts consuming tokens during periods of inactivity. The theft occurs through the extraction of session cookies from browser environments, which are then used to bypass multi-factor authentication protocols. Data logs indicate that compromised accounts are being utilized to facilitate high-volume automated queries, resulting in thousands of dollars in unexpected charges for impacted subscribers. Anthropic is currently auditing API usage patterns to identify accounts exhibiting anomalous traffic spikes indicative of unauthorized token consumption.

On the infrastructure front, hackers are targeting F5 Big-IP Access Policy Manager devices. The exploit involves a Linux rootkit that intercepts PHP file loading to inject a fileless web shell directly into memory, which allows the malicious code to persist without writing to the disk. This method effectively bypasses traditional integrity checks that rely on file system monitoring and hash verification. The vulnerability affects multiple versions of the Big-IP software suite currently deployed in enterprise environments. By remaining resident in system memory, the malicious payload continues to operate even after a system reboot, complicating the incident response process for network administrators.

The space industry sees a major shift as The Exploration Company has raised four hundred and fifty million dollars in a Series C round. This is the largest funding round to date for a European space company and will support the development of reusable spacecraft aimed at competing with existing launch providers. The capital will specifically fund the manufacturing of the Nyx Earth vehicle, which is designed to transport up to four thousand kilograms of cargo to the International Space Station. This funding round follows the company’s successful demonstration of its capsule reentry technology, which is intended to reduce the cost per kilogram of orbital transport by forty percent compared to current market standards.

In manufacturing, the United Kingdom based startup CloudNC has secured twenty million dollars in a funding extension. The company develops software to automate production bottlenecks, bringing its total capital raised to one hundred and twenty-eight million dollars. CloudNC utilizes proprietary algorithms to generate computer-aided manufacturing code directly from three-dimensional computer-aided design files, eliminating the need for manual programming by human machinists. The startup reports that its software reduces the lead time for precision-engineered parts by approximately seventy-five percent. This additional capital will be used to expand its facility in Essex and scale its machine shop operations to support aerospace and defense contracts.

Finally, LG smart televisions have been identified scanning local area networks for connected third-party devices, including smartphones. The activity occurs even when the televisions are in standby mode or disconnected from the internet. Security audits reveal that these devices broadcast discovery packets to identify all hardware sharing the local subnet, including printers, routers, and mobile handsets. This network scanning behavior is tied to the proprietary operating system’s integration with home automation protocols, which collect device identifiers and signal strength data. The gathered information is cached locally on the television’s storage until an active network connection is reestablished, at which point the telemetry data is transmitted to the manufacturer’s servers.
