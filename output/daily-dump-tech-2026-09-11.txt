It is September 11, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

OpenAI has temporarily paused new Pro subscriptions for its services. The company cites significant demand for the Astra model, which has strained existing infrastructure and prompted a halt in sign-ups while additional capacity is brought online. This pause affects both individual and team-tier subscriptions, blocking access to the Astra platform’s multi-modal capabilities. The current server load exceeds the capacity of the company’s existing H-100 GPU clusters, which have been operating at maximum utilization rates since the model’s wide release last week.

In the developer space, Cognition has launched its new software engineering model, SWE-2. The release aims to compete with existing tools like Fable five-point-one and OpenAI's Astra, focusing on autonomous repository-level coding tasks. Unlike its predecessor, SWE-1, which required manual intervention for complex debugging, SWE-2 executes end-to-end tasks, including local environment setup and pull request generation. Benchmarks from Cognition indicate the model achieves a forty-two percent success rate on the internal SWE-bench verification suite, a fourteen percent increase over the previous generation.

Slack is rolling out a new feature set called Slackforce Surfaces that integrates directly into chat interfaces. Users can generate interactive reports, dashboards, and polls by prompting the bot to aggregate information from ongoing conversations. These surfaces function as persistent modules that update in real-time as new messages arrive in a channel, replacing static summary threads. The feature pulls data from Slack’s internal data warehouse to visualize trends, such as volume of support tickets or project milestone completion rates.

Universal Music Group is entering the generative AI market through a multiyear licensing deal with ElevenLabs. The platform will allow users to create remixes and mashups using the label's catalog of licensed music. This partnership establishes a royalty-sharing structure where artists receive a percentage of revenue generated from content created using their voice or instrumental stems. The deal covers over four million tracks from the Universal catalog, marking a shift from the label's previous policy of issuing takedown notices to AI developers for unauthorized training on their intellectual property.

Turning to hardware manufacturing, the fusion energy startup Proxima Fusion announced plans to build a one-hundred-forty-million-euro factory for high-temperature superconducting tape. This facility aims to reduce reliance on Asian suppliers for the critical components used in their reactor designs. The factory, located in Saxony, Germany, is scheduled to reach full production capacity by late 2028, producing approximately two hundred kilometers of superconducting tape per year. This output is designed to supply the internal requirements for three full-scale stellarator reactors currently in the planning phase.

On the mobile carrier front, T-Mobile is introducing a five-dollar monthly surcharge for the iPhone Handoff feature. This allows the same phone number to be active on two separate iPhones using e-SIM technology, a service currently limited to T-Mobile in the United States and Deutsche Telekom in Germany. The charge applies to all primary lines that activate a secondary device on the same billing account. This represents a departure from the carrier’s previous model, which allowed multi-device e-SIM activation at no additional cost for subscribers on premium unlimited plans.

In security, IDScan is providing free credit monitoring and identity protection services to affected users following a leak of driver's license data. The breach was recently tied to an ongoing federal investigation into a dark web marketplace. Approximately twelve million records were compromised, containing full names, residential addresses, and high-resolution images of government-issued identification. IDScan has stated that the vulnerability originated in a legacy database server that had not received critical security patches since early 2024.

Finally, Amazon is discounting the Fire TV Stick 4K through September thirteenth. The device, which features Wi-Fi six support and two gigabytes of memory, is currently available for sixteen dollars and nine cents at select retailers. This price represents a sixty-eight percent reduction from the standard retail price of fifty dollars. The current inventory liquidation precedes the expected release of a new hardware iteration, which is scheduled to incorporate an upgraded quad-core processor and support for higher-bitrate streaming codecs.
