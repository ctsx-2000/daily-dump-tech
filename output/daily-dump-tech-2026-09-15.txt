OpenAI has acquired smartphone camera manufacturer Glass Imaging for three hundred million dollars. The startup was founded by former Apple engineers responsible for the development of Portrait Mode, and the acquisition brings that imaging expertise directly in-house for OpenAI. Glass Imaging’s proprietary computational photography algorithms utilize neural networks to perform image reconstruction, replacing the traditional hardware-limited lens stack with software-defined enhancement. This move integrates the startup’s team of forty engineers, who previously optimized camera performance for the iPhone 12 through iPhone 16 series, directly into OpenAI’s multimodal research division to improve the visual processing capabilities of its upcoming hardware initiatives.

Apple has begun integrating support for third-party AI models into its operating systems. New private frameworks within iOS twenty-seven and macOS Golden Gate allow the Siri architecture to be swapped out for external providers including Claude and ChatGPT. This transition shifts Apple’s strategy from its previous reliance on the on-device Siri engine to a modular model-agnostic layer. Users can now toggle between different large language models as the system backend, enabling these third-party agents to access system-level permissions such as calendar management, file retrieval, and cross-application data synthesis that were previously restricted to native Apple processes.

Nvidia Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang has addressed concerns regarding the potential slowing of AI development. During a public exchange, Huang stated that the company will not facilitate an industry-wide slowdown, distinguishing his position from other tech executives who have recently called for a pause to pace the frontier of artificial intelligence. Nvidia’s current production roadmap remains fixed on the Blackwell-successor architecture, which maintains a two-year release cadence. Huang confirmed that the company is currently fulfilling orders for its latest clusters at a rate exceeding five billion dollars in quarterly revenue, citing high demand from sovereign cloud providers and independent research laboratories as the primary drivers for continued rapid deployment.

In the infrastructure sector, Cornelis has raised two hundred and five million dollars to address latency issues in GPU clusters. The company simultaneously launched Active Compute Fabric, a network technology designed to mitigate bottlenecks caused by data transfer delays between processors. This fabric replaces standard Ethernet-based interconnects with a proprietary protocol that reduces inter-node communication latency by sixty-five percent during large-scale model training. The funding round, led by institutional venture capital firms, brings the total capital raised by Cornelis to four hundred and eighty million dollars, with the new resources earmarked for expanding production capacity for the Active Compute Fabric chips.

Italian startup Exein has closed a two hundred and seventy million dollar funding round at a one point seven billion dollar valuation. The company specializes in physical AI, focusing on the intersection of artificial intelligence and hardware security for industrial applications. Exein’s security suite monitors embedded firmware in real-time, replacing static signature-based detection with behavioral analysis models that identify unauthorized access attempts within industrial control systems. The firm currently services forty-five global manufacturing clients, and the new capital will support the scaling of its security operations centers across North America and East Asia.

On the defense front, the United States military has confirmed the deployment of space control weapons into orbit. These assets are categorized as defensive capabilities designed to protect the joint force in the orbital domain. The deployment consists of six maneuverable satellite units equipped with electronic warfare suites capable of jamming or spoofing incoming signals directed at American reconnaissance infrastructure. These units operate in low-earth orbit and function as a layer of active protection for the existing constellation of military communications satellites, which have reported a thirty percent increase in signal interference events over the last twelve months.

Finally, Mozilla has released a report detailing the narrowing gap between proprietary and open-source artificial intelligence. The findings indicate that open models have reached capability parity with frontier models while requiring significantly lower investment, effectively providing a four-month competitive head start for those utilizing paid proprietary systems. Mozilla analyzed twenty-two benchmark tasks across coding, reasoning, and creative writing, finding that the cost to train an open-source model with equivalent performance to the leading proprietary systems has decreased by eighty-two percent since the start of the year. The report concludes that the barrier to entry for high-performance model training has shifted from proprietary data access to the availability of specialized compute resources.
