The United States Department of Justice has finalized its antitrust filing against Google, formally requesting that the federal court compel the company to divest its Chrome web browser. This proposal stems from the court’s August ruling that Google maintains an illegal monopoly in the search market by leveraging its browser to secure default search placements. The filing asks the court to prohibit Google from entering into exclusive revenue-sharing agreements with browser developers like Mozilla and device manufacturers like Apple, which currently account for billions of dollars in annual payments. The Department of Justice intends to restrict Google from utilizing its Android platform to preference its own search products over competitors.

Microsoft has officially launched its Windows 365 Link, a dedicated thin-client device designed exclusively for cloud-based computing. The hardware features no local storage and no local operating system beyond a locked-down firmware layer, requiring a persistent connection to the Azure cloud environment. Priced at three hundred forty-nine dollars, the device is intended for enterprise environments where local data persistence is a security liability. It replaces traditional desktop hardware by offloading all processing tasks to remote server instances, utilizing a customized version of the Windows 365 interface. The device is scheduled for general availability in early 2025 across select enterprise markets.

OpenAI has released a new research version of its model, identified as Operator, which is designed to perform autonomous actions within a browser environment. The software is capable of executing tasks such as filling out forms, navigating websites, and performing data entry by controlling a user interface rather than merely generating text output. This development marks a transition from standard chat-based interactions to agentic workflows that interact with external web tools on behalf of the user. The release is currently limited to a developer preview through the company’s application programming interface, specifically targeting complex web-based automation tasks that previously required manual input.

Nvidia has reported its third-quarter fiscal results, showing revenue of thirty-five billion dollars, a ninety-four percent increase compared to the same period last year. The data center segment contributed thirty billion dollars of that total, driven largely by the high demand for the company’s Blackwell and Hopper graphics processing units. Production of the Blackwell architecture is now ramping up, with shipments scheduled to begin in the fourth quarter. The company’s gross margin reached seventy-four percent, reflecting the scale of enterprise and cloud provider investment in large-scale artificial intelligence training infrastructure.

Amazon has launched a new logistics service titled Haul, an online storefront shipping directly from warehouses in China to consumers in the United States. The service focuses on non-branded items priced under twenty dollars, with delivery times ranging from one to two weeks. This model bypasses traditional domestic fulfillment centers by shipping individual orders directly from overseas vendors to the customer, utilizing international air freight logistics. The platform is currently integrated into the existing Amazon mobile application, specifically targeting the market segment currently served by international discount retailers.

NASA and the European Space Agency have confirmed that the James Webb Space Telescope has detected the earliest known galaxy in the observable universe. Designated as JADES-GS-z14-zero, the galaxy formed approximately two hundred ninety million years after the Big Bang. Spectroscopic data indicates that the galaxy is significantly larger and brighter than previous theoretical models predicted for such an early stage of cosmic development. The detection was made possible by the telescope’s near-infrared camera, which identified light shifted into the infrared spectrum due to the expansion of the universe over the last thirteen billion years.

The European Union has moved forward with the implementation of the Digital Markets Act, issuing formal requests for information to several major technology platforms. Regulators are demanding detailed documentation regarding how these companies rank search results and provide access to user data for third-party advertisers. This action follows the designation of these companies as gatekeepers under the new regulatory framework, which mandates interoperability and data transparency. Companies found to be in violation of these mandates face potential fines of up to ten percent of their total worldwide annual turnover, increasing to twenty percent for repeated non-compliance.

Intel has announced the successful production of its latest 18A process node, with the first server processors based on this technology now in the testing phase. The node utilizes a new transistor architecture designed to increase performance-per-watt efficiency by approximately fifteen percent compared to the prior 20A process. This transition represents a shift in the company’s manufacturing strategy, moving toward high-volume extreme ultraviolet lithography for its logic chips. The processors are currently undergoing validation for enterprise server platforms, with mass production slated for the first half of the coming year.
