The Federal Communications Commission has waived its twenty-five percent foreign equity ownership limit for the Paramount Warner Brothers case. The ruling allows sovereign wealth funds from Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi to collectively own forty-nine point five percent of the company. This decision marks a significant departure from standard oversight protocols that have governed foreign media investment since the Telecommunications Act of 1934. The waiver process involved a sixteen-month review period during which the FCC evaluated the national security implications of the combined media conglomerate's cross-platform distribution networks.

On the hardware side, Snap has opened pre-orders for its standalone augmented reality glasses. The device operates independently without requiring a connection to a smartphone or computer, and Verizon is offering dedicated data plans for the glasses. These glasses utilize an internal custom silicon processor designed to handle spatial mapping and real-time rendering locally on the device rather than offloading tasks to a tethered handset. The unit weighs 142 grams and includes a battery pack rated for 90 minutes of continuous high-fidelity display usage, marking a shift from the previous tethered Spectacles 4 model.

California Governor Gavin Newsom has issued an executive order to establish state-level oversight for frontier artificial intelligence models. The order directs experts to develop recommendations for a mandatory kill switch that would allow the state to disable systems that pose significant risks. This directive applies to any model trained using more than 10 to the power of 26 floating-point operations. The proposed oversight framework requires developers to submit periodic safety audits and vulnerability assessments to the California Department of Technology, moving beyond the voluntary reporting structures currently utilized by federal agencies.

Turning to executive changes, Disney has appointed its first chief technology officer, Karandeep Anand. Anand previously served as the chief executive officer of Character AI. In this new role, Anand will oversee the integration of generative AI across Disney’s streaming, parks, and consumer products divisions, reporting directly to the chief executive officer. His appointment replaces the previous fragmented technical leadership structure where engineering heads operated independently within individual business units, centralizing control over the company’s multi-billion dollar digital infrastructure budget.

In the enterprise software market, data indicates that sixty-nine percent of organizations using OpenAI's agent platform designate it as their primary tool. Anthropic's platform is currently primary for thirty-eight percent of its corporate users, though it leads in total consideration among potential customers. These figures are based on a survey of 500 enterprise IT decision-makers managing infrastructure deployments exceeding 50 million dollars in annual spend. The data highlights a narrowing gap in market adoption as enterprises transition from experimental pilot programs to full-scale deployment of autonomous agent workflows within internal business operations.

Regarding federal infrastructure, the official United States Federal Register website briefly utilized an open-source Chinese artificial intelligence search tool. The specific model had previously been flagged by the Federal Bureau of Investigation as malicious. The tool was integrated into the site’s search functionality via an external plugin that bypassed standard cybersecurity vetting protocols for government domains. Federal technicians removed the tool within four hours of discovery, but not before the model had indexed approximately 12,000 pages of sensitive regulatory documents and internal government communications during its brief period of active service.

Finally, a research report indicates that the increased availability of artificial intelligence chatbots is accelerating the discovery of software vulnerabilities. While industry labs discuss voluntary agreements to slow development, these tools are concurrently being used to uncover a high volume of security flaws across commercial codebases. Analysts observed a 42 percent increase in unique vulnerability disclosures over the last six months compared to the same period in 2025. This surge is attributed to the deployment of specialized large language models capable of automated static analysis that identifies zero-day flaws ten times faster than traditional manual auditing methods used by security researchers.
