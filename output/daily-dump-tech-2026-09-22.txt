It is September 22, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

Adobe has released an Android version of its Premiere video-editing software. The application includes layout optimizations for both standard candy bar phones and foldable devices and is available for free download starting today. This release brings the primary mobile iteration of Premiere to parity with the iOS version, which previously dominated Adobe’s mobile video suite. The software integrates directly with Creative Cloud, allowing users to move projects between mobile devices and desktop workstations without manual file transfers. It replaces the legacy Premiere Rush mobile app, offering a significantly expanded feature set including multi-track timeline editing, AI-powered color grading, and native support for 8K video rendering. Adobe estimates the Android tablet and foldable market segment accounts for approximately 40 percent of its mobile-creative user base, a demographic that previously relied on third-party alternatives for professional-grade editing.

On the hardware side, Oppo has launched its Find X10 Pro Max flagship smartphone in China. The device features a three-camera array, with each lens utilizing a two-hundred-megapixel sensor. The main camera supports seventeen stops of dynamic range through a proprietary sensor technology called DeepPix. This sensor architecture marks a shift from the fifteen stops offered in the previous X9 series, effectively doubling the light-gathering capability in high-contrast environments. The phone is powered by the latest octa-core chipset, which features a 15 percent increase in thermal efficiency over the previous model. The unit includes a 6,000 milliamp-hour silicon-carbon battery, a 10 percent capacity increase over the predecessor, while maintaining the same physical chassis dimensions.

In semiconductor manufacturing, the Singapore-based firm Nexstrom has secured 450 million dollars in Series D funding to scale its production equipment. The company is developing tools specifically designed to manufacture two-dimensional semiconductor materials at scale for chip fabrication plants. These tools are engineered to deposit transition metal dichalcogenide thin films onto 300-millimeter wafers, a process that replaces traditional silicon etching for specific high-frequency logic components. The funding will support the construction of a new manufacturing facility in Jurong, capable of producing sixty deposition systems per year. This output aims to meet the demand from major foundry operators currently transitioning to sub-two-nanometer process nodes, which require these specialized materials to manage electron mobility and heat dissipation.

Turning to security, the recently released Meta AI agent known as Muse contains a zero-day vulnerability. The flaw allows for a remote hijack via a ClickFix attack, which can be used to gain control over the agent. By manipulating the interface’s permission requests, an unauthorized actor can bypass the agent's safety guardrails to execute arbitrary system commands. Meta reports that the vulnerability affects all versions of the Muse agent deployed within the last three weeks, impacting approximately 2.4 million active enterprise accounts. The attack vector exploits the way the agent processes asynchronous data streams, allowing the payload to execute before the user can verify the origin of the command.

A fiber optic cable cut in New Jersey caused a widespread outage for the Federal Aviation Administration on Monday. Construction crews severed the line, leading to a circuit failure that resulted in hundreds of flight cancellations and delays across the United States. The damaged cable was part of a redundant high-speed backbone connecting the FAA’s primary data center in New Jersey to its regional air traffic control facilities. The outage lasted four hours, affecting the Notice to Air Missions system, which provides real-time safety information to pilots. Data indicates that 450 flights were canceled and 1,200 were delayed as a direct result of the connectivity loss, forcing controllers to revert to manual coordination protocols.

Finally, the original creator of the Windows Task Manager, Dave Plummer, has released a cross-platform version of the utility. The new iteration is built to run on both macOS and Linux, offering users the same process management capabilities outside of the Windows environment. The utility, rewritten in a memory-safe language, replicates the original hierarchical process view and resource monitoring metrics that were first introduced in Windows NT 4.0. It supports real-time CPU, memory, and disk usage tracking for all active processes, providing granular control over thread prioritization and background service termination. The tool is distributed under an open-source license, providing a direct alternative to native system monitors like Activity Monitor on macOS and the top or htop utilities on Linux, while maintaining a consistent visual interface across all three operating systems.
