It is September 23, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

OpenAI is moving into creator services, hiring three key executives from Patreon. Among them is co-founder Sam Yam, who joins to lead the company’s new creator product division. The move signals a shift in OpenAI's strategy toward building specialized tools for content creators. The company aims to integrate its generative models directly into workflows currently dominated by creator economy platforms that manage subscription payments and community engagement. Yam previously oversaw product development at Patreon, where he managed a platform supporting over two hundred thousand creators and millions of recurring subscribers.

On the audio streaming front, Spotify is rolling out a feature called Taste Profile to Premium users in the United States. This tool provides users a view into the data the platform uses for its recommendation algorithm. It also introduces a natural language interface that allows listeners to request specific changes to their personalized music streams. The interface replaces the manual adjustment of genre sliders, allowing users to input text prompts to filter by tempo, mood, or specific era. Spotify processes over one hundred billion data points daily to determine its recommendation logic, and this rollout grants users visibility into the top fifty tags currently influencing their personalized discovery feeds.

In enterprise AI, the startup Ema has raised seventy-seven million dollars in fresh capital. The company, which specializes in AI agents for business services, has now secured one hundred and forty million dollars total. It currently counts Google and Microsoft among its fifty enterprise customers. Ema’s platform deploys generative agents that integrate with existing enterprise software suites, including Salesforce and Workday, to automate data retrieval and document summarization. The company reports that its agents have already processed over five million internal documents across its client base, significantly reducing the average time required for cross-departmental information audits.

Turning to security, Microsoft has disrupted a platform known as EvilTokens that was used to facilitate AI-assisted account compromises. The service provided end-to-end infrastructure for mass attacks, affecting roughly twelve thousand accounts. Microsoft's intervention disabled the platform's ability to automate these malicious logins. EvilTokens functioned by intercepting session cookies, a method that allows attackers to bypass multi-factor authentication by mimicking a verified browser state. The service had been active for six months, during which time it sold automated login scripts to over three hundred individual threat actors for a monthly subscription fee.

LinkedIn is expanding its efforts to combat platform fraud with new verification tools. Users can now utilize these features to vouch for the work history of their colleagues. The update also grants companies more administrative control to identify and remove accounts that falsely claim to represent their organization. LinkedIn will now cross-reference employment claims against verified corporate email domains and payroll partner databases to flag discrepancies. This update arrives as the platform reports a twenty percent increase in the volume of automated bot accounts attempting to scrape professional networking data over the last fiscal quarter.

Rabbit is decoupling its AI software from its R1 hardware. The company is launching a standalone agent, titled O-S-3, which operates as a cloud-based service. It is designed to run locally across Windows, Mac, and Linux environments, removing the requirement to use the startup's physical device. The R1 device, which launched earlier this year, faced significant criticism for its limited battery life and reliance on a proprietary hardware interface that lacked standard peripheral support. O-S-3 utilizes the same large action model architecture as the R1, allowing it to navigate graphical user interfaces and execute complex sequences across desktop applications without manual user intervention.

Finally, Eight Sleep has unveiled the Pod Six, the latest iteration of its smart mattress cover. The system monitors health metrics including heart rate and breathing to automatically adjust bed temperature throughout the night. The company describes this as its most significant hardware launch to date, with the new cooling hub designed to fit underneath standard bed frames. The Pod Six features a redesigned hydro-cooling engine that operates at forty percent lower noise levels than the Pod Five. It utilizes updated biometric sensors that increase data sampling frequency to one hundred hertz, providing a granular view of sleep architecture that is processed through the company's proprietary thermal regulation software.
