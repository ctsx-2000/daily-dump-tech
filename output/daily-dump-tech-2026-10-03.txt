OpenAI is rolling out new shopping features for ChatGPT that allow users to virtually try on clothing and accessories. The integration uses personal photos to map items onto the user and includes a library feature to save and track preferred products. This update expands upon the existing Vision capabilities, moving beyond static image analysis to dynamic 3D asset overlays. The platform processes these images through a latent diffusion model that aligns retail inventory data with the user’s specific body dimensions captured in the uploaded photos. Users can now filter search results by brand availability and real-time stock levels, which replaces the manual browsing experience previously required when navigating third-party e-commerce platforms.

Moving to game development, Capcom announced plans to pivot its proprietary RE Engine into an AI-generation game engine. The company intends to gradually integrate generative tools into its production pipeline to automate asset creation for future titles. This transition shifts the focus from manual 3D modeling and high-fidelity texturing to prompt-based environment generation. The RE Engine, which debuted in 2017 with Resident Evil 7, will now incorporate a generative layer capable of synthesizing character animations and environmental lighting maps from text prompts. This integration aims to reduce the development cycle for large-scale open-world maps, which historically required thousands of man-hours to populate with individual static assets.

On the automotive front, Rivian reported its third-quarter production and delivery numbers today, showing significant growth. The company produced nineteen thousand seven hundred fifty-one vehicles and delivered nineteen thousand two hundred forty-eight, marking an eighty-five percent increase compared to the same period last year. These figures reflect the expanded output from the Normal, Illinois manufacturing plant following the integration of the R2 platform production lines. The company currently reports a total backlog of over sixty thousand orders, with the R1S model accounting for fifty-four percent of the quarterly deliveries.

In the legal sector, Lyft has finalized a settlement in a long-running driver misclassification lawsuit for two hundred seventy-two point five million dollars. The agreement addresses claims regarding the employment status of drivers, though the settlement does not reclassify them as full-time employees under the current terms. This payment concludes litigation that spanned five years and involved claims from over forty thousand drivers across multiple jurisdictions. The funds will be distributed based on hours logged and miles driven during the class period, maintaining the existing independent contractor framework while providing back-pay for certain operational expenses.

Regarding artificial intelligence infrastructure, Meta has released the source code for its Muse agent platform for free. The company is encouraging third-party developers to integrate the agent across various hardware categories, including home appliances and smart displays. Muse is designed as a multimodal system capable of processing local sensor data without requiring a persistent cloud connection for basic command execution. The released package includes the core inference engine and a library of lightweight neural network weights, allowing manufacturers to deploy AI functionality on hardware with limited onboard memory, such as smart refrigerators and connected thermostats.

In console gaming, Sony is adding AI-based graphics upscaling to the standard PlayStation five. The new feature, called Quick Spectral Super Resolution, improves image detail and stability, with initial support launching for the titles Marvel's Wolverine and Ghost of Yotei. This system utilizes a dedicated machine learning processor within the console to reconstruct high-resolution frames from a lower internal rendering resolution. It serves as a hardware-level replacement for traditional anti-aliasing techniques, allowing for a consistent sixty frames per second performance target in titles that previously fluctuated between thirty and forty frames per second.

Finally, a security audit of seventy-three healthcare facilities revealed that forty percent failed a patient-zero simulation. The drill, which tested physical and digital security protocols, found that actors posing as patients were able to access restricted facility areas or sensitive systems in nearly half of the tested locations. The audit tracked the ability of unauthorized personnel to gain entry to server rooms, pharmacy storage, and terminal-access kiosks within administrative wings. Of the thirty facilities that failed the audit, twenty-two identified the vulnerability as a failure in biometric badge authentication, while the remainder cited weak credentialing for guest-access networks that bridged into clinical record databases.
