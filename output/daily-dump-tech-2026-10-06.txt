Mistral has released its new trillion-parameter AI model, codenamed Le Chonk. The company states this is its most capable open-weight offering to date, designed to compete directly with frontier-level models developed by major labs. This model utilizes a sparse mixture-of-experts architecture, requiring 400 gigabytes of VRAM for inference at full precision. It succeeds the Mistral Large 2 release, which featured 123 billion parameters and a 128,000 token context window.

In the neurotech space, a new startup called Bridge Neurotech has launched to compete with existing brain-computer interface companies. Unlike current systems, Bridge is developing a wearable interface that utilizes ultrasound technology rather than invasive sensors or electromagnetic signals. The device captures hemodynamic responses in the cerebral cortex with a spatial resolution of two millimeters, avoiding the need for craniotomy procedures required by Neuralink’s N1 implant. This approach replaces traditional electroencephalography sensors that are typically limited by signal noise and scalp impedance issues.

A recent industry survey indicates that 90 percent of VMware users are actively exploring alternative platforms. The primary drivers for this migration are rising licensing costs and a desire to mitigate supply chain disruption risks. Since Broadcom’s acquisition, subscription pricing for the VMware Cloud Foundation bundle has increased by an average of 400 percent for enterprise clients. Data centers are shifting workloads to Nutanix, Proxmox, and OpenStack to replace the vSphere hypervisor and vCenter management environments.

Security researchers have identified significant risks in the Model Context Protocol, or MCP, which is used for agent-to-agent communication. The protocol lacks sufficient trust validation, allowing malicious prompts to propagate between AI agents during automated tasks. Testing shows that compromised agents can inject unauthorized instructions into downstream workflows, bypassing existing context-length security filters. The vulnerability affects all current integrations between large language models and external tool-use environments that rely on unencrypted MCP handshakes.

JetBrains has reported a net financial loss for the first time in its tracked corporate history. The company, which provides a suite of professional developer tools, has not disclosed specific reasons for the decline in profitability. Public filings show a 15 percent decrease in annual recurring revenue from its flagship integrated development environments. This marks a shift from the previous five-year trend, where the firm consistently maintained an average annual growth rate of 20 percent.

Uber is investing 2.3 billion dollars to expand its operations into the catering market. This move is part of a broader strategy to scale the Uber Eats platform beyond individual meal delivery into high-volume corporate and event logistics. The capital allocation focuses on building a dedicated fulfillment fleet and specialized enterprise software for managing orders exceeding five hundred units. This division aims to capture market share currently held by legacy catering management platforms and dedicated corporate hospitality services.

The Nobel Prize in Physiology has been awarded to the researchers responsible for developing optogenetics. This technique, which uses light to control brain cells, originated from the study of light-seeking algae and has become a foundational tool in modern neuroscience. By introducing light-sensitive opsin proteins into specific neuron populations, researchers can trigger or inhibit neural activity with millisecond precision using fiber-optic light delivery. The technology replaces legacy pharmacological methods that previously lacked the temporal resolution required to map complex circuit behaviors in the mammalian brain.

Finally, a city in Texas is demanding two million dollars in fees for public records regarding police usage of Flock surveillance technology. The request highlights increasing municipal efforts to restrict public access to data on automated license plate reader deployment. The city cited the costs of redacting proprietary metadata and legal review as the basis for the multi-million dollar assessment. This represents a 5,000 percent increase over standard administrative fees typically charged for municipal record requests in the state.
