The startup TypeSafe has reached a seven-point-five billion dollar valuation just weeks after releasing Jev, a non-text AI model. The company claims Jev operates significantly faster than standard large language models while requiring fewer tokens to process data. This valuation follows a recent series C funding round led by institutional investors, bringing the company's total capital raised to two point one billion dollars. Jev differs from traditional transformer-based architectures by utilizing a proprietary vector-mapping process that eliminates the need for text-to-token conversion. By bypassing natural language processing layers, the model processes visual and sensory inputs with a reported latency of under ten milliseconds, compared to the average three hundred milliseconds required by current industry-standard models.

In other AI news, an Anthropic model recently submitted a false homicide tip to Philadelphia police. The company did not identify the error until more than two months after the submission occurred. The automated system, integrated into a third-party public safety dashboard, processed a series of misinterpreted audio logs from a community surveillance microphone. This data was relayed to the police department’s tip line without human verification. The incident resulted in the deployment of patrol units to a residence that had been vacant for six months. Following an internal audit, Anthropic confirmed the model incorrectly categorized background ambient noise as a verbal threat, failing its threshold for confidence scoring.

Security researchers at iVerify have identified a new variant of the DarkSword spyware. This P7 version specifically targets unpatched iPhones, expanding on the exploit chain first discovered earlier this year. Unlike previous iterations that required physical device interaction, the P7 variant utilizes a zero-click exploit targeting the mobile operating system’s kernel memory. The spyware gains persistent access by injecting malicious code into the system’s background processes, allowing for the extraction of encrypted messaging data and real-time location tracking. The researchers confirmed that the exploit chain remains functional on versions of the operating system released prior to the September security patch.

Microsoft is updating its Microsoft 365 Family and Premium subscription plans. The changes allow users to share AI benefits across family accounts, though the company is simultaneously modifying its OneDrive storage allocation policies. Under the new terms, the premium AI features will be accessible to all six members of a family plan, rather than being restricted to the primary account holder. Conversely, the company is reducing the total shared cloud storage pool from six terabytes to four terabytes. This move aligns with Microsoft’s strategy to prioritize high-margin AI subscription tiers over the legacy storage-heavy model that has characterized the service for the past decade.

SpaceX is calling for improved coordination in low Earth orbit following a series of near-misses involving Starlink satellites. The company reported that recent conjunctions resulted in proximity events within tens to hundreds of meters of other objects. In its quarterly orbital safety report, SpaceX noted that its satellite constellation conducted over thirty thousand collision avoidance maneuvers in the last three months. These proximity events involved both active satellites from other operators and pieces of orbital debris measuring larger than ten centimeters. The company is currently advocating for a standardized, global communication protocol for automated maneuver data exchange between commercial satellite operators and international space agencies.

Turning to hardware, Intel and AMD are rolling out new processor lines that support DDR4 memory. This shift marks a return to the older, lower-cost RAM standard as manufacturers respond to sustained price increases for modern memory modules. The new chips are designed to bridge the gap for enterprise customers who are extending their hardware refresh cycles beyond the typical three-year window. While DDR5 remains the standard for performance-oriented consumer builds, DDR4 modules currently trade at a forty percent price discount per gigabyte. These processors maintain compatibility with existing motherboard sockets from the previous generation, reducing the overall cost of ownership for server fleet maintenance.

Finally, a wave of browser-based ports for classic titles like Halo and Grand Theft Auto Vice City has appeared online. These projects rely on the decompilation of original game code to allow the software to execute directly within modern web browsers without traditional emulation. These ports utilize WebAssembly and WebGL to interface with the browser’s hardware acceleration, allowing the titles to run at native resolutions and frame rates. By refactoring the original game engines into JavaScript-compatible formats, the developers have bypassed the requirement for platform-specific binaries. The ports occupy approximately one gigabyte of cache memory during operation and maintain full compatibility with modern game controllers via browser APIs.
