Microsoft has ended support for Windows eleven twenty-four H two Home and Pro editions. These versions will no longer receive monthly security or non-security preview updates. This marks the end of the twenty-four-month lifecycle for this specific feature release, which originally launched in October of 2024. Systems currently running this build are now vulnerable to new exploits that will be patched in subsequent versions like twenty-five H two, which users are now forced to migrate toward to maintain a supported security posture.

On the hardware side, Amazon has launched two new AI-powered devices under its Bee brand. The Bee Frontier is an updated wearable, and the new Bee Recorder is a credit-card-sized device that syncs with smartphones to summarize conversations and manage tasks. The Bee Recorder features a proprietary neural processing unit capable of local audio transcription, replacing the need for cloud-based processing of sensitive meeting data. The Frontier wearable integrates a new biometric sensor array with forty percent higher accuracy than the previous generation, specifically targeting blood oxygen and heart rate variability tracking.

Google is integrating a universal shopping cart into its Gemini app and search results in the United States. The tool allows users to track and manage items across different retailers within the Google interface ahead of the holiday shopping season. This functionality aggregates data from over five hundred thousand participating merchants, centralizing cart management that previously required visiting individual retailer websites. The system utilizes real-time API integrations to pull inventory levels and pricing fluctuations directly into the Google interface.

In security, researchers at The Citizen Lab have identified a campaign distributing zero-click spyware through targeted online advertisements. The exploit was used to compromise devices at the news organization Al Jazeera after users were served malicious ads. The attack vector bypassed standard browser security protocols by leveraging a previously unknown vulnerability in the ad-rendering engine. The spyware successfully exfiltrated encrypted messaging logs and geolocation data from mobile devices without requiring any user interaction beyond loading the compromised webpage.

Figma has announced new features to define how agents function on its design platform. The company also released its code layers feature into open beta for all users. The agent framework allows design teams to automate repetitive layout tasks by defining constraints that mirror the structural logic of production-ready web components. The code layers feature provides a direct translation layer between visual design elements and CSS architecture, reducing the manual handoff time between designers and engineering teams by an average of thirty percent.

Turning to the consulting sector, McKinsey and Company is implementing an AI simulation as part of its interview process. Entry-level candidates will participate in a ten-minute conversation with an AI client designed to replicate eighty percent of real-world consulting scenarios. The simulation assesses candidates on logical reasoning, data synthesis, and communication clarity under time constraints. This automated assessment replaces the first round of human-led case interviews, standardizing the evaluation criteria across all global applicants.

Finally, a new streaming platform called Maka has launched to address the prevalence of low-quality AI-generated content in children's media. The service is managed by child development experts and focuses on curated content as an alternative to automated media. The platform utilizes a human-only verification process for all uploaded videos, explicitly banning content generated by large language models or automated video synthesis tools. The library features five thousand hours of hand-animated and live-action programming, specifically vetted to meet developmental benchmarks for children aged three to ten.
