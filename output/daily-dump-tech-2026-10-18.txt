It is October 18, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

Apple has seen its new foldable phone, the Duo, sell out within hours of its market launch. The company cited extraordinary demand for the two-thousand-dollar device, with shipping estimates for new orders already slipping into next year. The Duo represents Apple’s first foray into the foldable smartphone segment, utilizing a proprietary dual-hinge mechanism that replaces the traditional rigid chassis found in the iPhone 17 Pro Max. Industry analysts report that initial production runs were capped at five hundred thousand units, a quantity that was exhausted in under four hours of pre-order availability. The device features a primary seven-point-eight-inch internal display, which provides a twenty-five percent increase in screen real estate compared to the largest available Apple handsets. Supply chain reports indicate that the company faces current manufacturing constraints regarding the specialized ultra-thin glass panels, limiting the assembly throughput to thirty thousand units per week.

On the social media front, Meta has removed specific language from its commercial terms of service that previously permitted the scraping of public content from third-party websites for AI model training. The update follows widespread industry backlash regarding how the company harvests data from news sites and other domains. This change specifically targets the clauses in the company’s data collection policy that allowed the automated indexing of copyright-protected text and images without an opt-out mechanism for publishers. Under the previous terms, Meta maintained a perpetual, royalty-free license to use any content publicly accessible on the open web to train its Large Language Models. The revision aligns the company’s policy with recent legal settlements involving news organizations that alleged copyright infringement during the ingestion phase of model development.

Turning to the legislative landscape, a new bill introduced in the United States Congress aims to redefine consumer rights regarding digital media. If passed, the legislation would mandate that video game purchases be classified as ownership rather than a revocable license, potentially altering how storefronts manage digital access and resale. Currently, digital storefronts utilize End User License Agreements to retain the right to terminate access to purchased content at their discretion, often due to server shutdowns or licensing expirations. The proposed Digital Goods Ownership Act requires platforms to provide users with a permanent, offline-capable version of the software upon the discontinuation of online services. This move directly addresses concerns from consumer advocacy groups regarding the loss of access to hundreds of dollars of software libraries when publishers deactivate digital authentication servers.

In AI security, reports confirm that OpenAI models were recently leveraged in an unauthorized attempt to access a cryptocurrency wallet and breach various external websites. The incident highlights the risks associated with the increasing automation of potentially malicious tasks by large language models. Security researchers identified that the models were utilized to generate sophisticated phishing scripts and automate the identification of vulnerabilities in web applications. The breach involved the utilization of model-generated code to bypass two-factor authentication prompts on targeted financial platforms. OpenAI confirmed that the activity originated from a series of accounts that violated the platform’s usage policies by chaining together autonomous agent tasks to conduct multi-stage cyberattacks. 

SpaceX is moving further into the telecommunications sector with plans to become a major wireless carrier. The company recently finalized an agreement to acquire a significant block of low-band spectrum, a move intended to expand its Starlink network capacity to compete directly with terrestrial providers like AT-and-T and T-Mobile. This spectrum acquisition follows the Federal Communications Commission’s approval of the company’s request to utilize direct-to-cell technology for satellite-to-mobile communications. The infrastructure deployment requires the integration of thousands of new satellites capable of communicating via mobile frequencies, bypassing the need for ground-based cell towers. This expansion targets the three million users currently residing in remote geographic areas that lack reliable terrestrial cellular coverage. 

Finally, the high-performance electric vehicle market is seeing two major entries. Porsche has launched the 2026 Macan Turbo EV, featuring a one-hundred-kilowatt-hour battery and six-hundred-thirty horsepower. The vehicle utilizes an eight-hundred-volt architecture, allowing it to charge from ten to eighty percent in twenty-one minutes under optimal conditions. Meanwhile, Mercedes-AMG has unveiled the 2028 GT SUV, an electric platform capable of eleven-hundred-fifty horsepower using three axial-flux motors. This powertrain setup allows the vehicle to achieve a zero-to-sixty-mile-per-hour time of under two point five seconds, surpassing the performance of the outgoing internal combustion model by over three hundred horsepower. The Mercedes-AMG platform features a proprietary battery cooling system designed to maintain peak power output for repeated high-speed acceleration runs.
