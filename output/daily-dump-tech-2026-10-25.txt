It is October 25, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

Block has released a new voice model capable of running locally on a MacBook M4 Max. The model performs speech synthesis tasks without requiring cloud connectivity, aiming for low-latency, private processing on consumer hardware. The model achieves inference speeds of under fifty milliseconds, replacing traditional server-side API calls that previously relied on high-bandwidth data centers. By utilizing the unified memory architecture of the M4 chip, the model processes audio streams at a rate of four hundred tokens per second, effectively eliminating the typical three-hundred-millisecond network round-trip latency associated with cloud-based speech synthesis.

On the regulatory front, Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro has signed the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act into law. The mandate requires clear, conspicuous disclosures on any advertisement—including still images, audio, or video—that has been significantly modified or generated by artificial intelligence. The law imposes civil penalties of up to ten thousand dollars per violation for companies that fail to include a persistent watermark or textual disclaimer. This legislation applies to all commercial digital media distributed within state borders, targeting platforms that display automated content to residents, regardless of the primary location of the content creator.

In critical security, developers have addressed two major vulnerabilities in the PHP programming language. These bugs posed systemic risks to the majority of web infrastructure, as they allowed for potential remote code execution on servers running affected versions of the runtime. The vulnerabilities impacted over seventy-five percent of websites globally that utilize PHP as a server-side scripting engine. The patches resolve flaws in the way the engine handles multipart form data, which previously allowed unauthorized parties to bypass existing input filters and gain elevated administrative access to the underlying server environment.

Turning to the business sector, Instacart has held discussions regarding a potential sale of the company. Reports indicate the grocery delivery platform engaged in talks with major competitors, including DoorDash and Uber, to explore acquisition possibilities. Instacart currently maintains a market share of approximately twenty-five percent in the United States grocery delivery sector, processing over three hundred million orders annually. The discussions follow a period of stagnant growth, with the company seeking to consolidate its logistics network and customer database with larger platforms that operate across both food delivery and retail fulfillment sectors.

Shifting to science, researchers have identified a one-thousand-mile-long cloud on Mars that may host physical processes never before observed in nature. The data suggests the formation involves ice-related physics that deviate from established atmospheric models found on Earth. Located above the Arsia Mons volcano, the cloud persists for nearly three hours each morning, reaching altitudes of over twelve miles. Unlike terrestrial water-based clouds, this formation relies on the condensation of carbon dioxide ice crystals in a low-pressure environment, creating a distinct refractive signature that prevents the cloud from dissipating as rapidly as standard planetary weather patterns would dictate.

Finally, reports confirm that Starlink satellite internet service has returned to normal operations in Ukraine. This follows a period where access was throttled after directives were issued to limit usage in the region. The restoration of full bandwidth follows the deployment of twenty additional low-earth orbit satellites specifically positioned to cover the Eastern European theater. The network now provides consistent throughput of two hundred megabits per second for authorized terminals, restoring connectivity levels to those seen prior to the imposition of regional data caps that had previously restricted throughput to below five megabits per second for non-governmental users.
