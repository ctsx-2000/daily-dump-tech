Federal investigators are looking into a suspected Chinese intelligence plot aimed at the chief executive officer of Nvidia. The investigation centers on a Singapore-based cloud computing company accused of illegally facilitating chip sales to China, which authorities now suspect may have served as a front for espionage activities. Prosecutors allege the firm circumvented United States export controls by rerouting thousands of high-end graphics processing units through shell entities in Southeast Asia. This case follows a series of Department of Commerce warnings regarding the illicit shipment of hardware designed for large-scale model training. The investigation now seeks to determine if senior leadership at the firm coordinated with foreign intelligence agencies to intercept proprietary data regarding Nvidia’s future product roadmaps.

Anthropic has filed its S-1 registration statement with the Securities and Exchange Commission, officially signaling its intent to go public on the Nasdaq. The filing provides the first public look at the company's internal financial data and operational scale as it seeks to raise capital in the competitive foundation model market. Anthropic reported an annual recurring revenue of 2.4 billion dollars for the fiscal year ending September 30, representing a 200 percent increase over the previous twelve-month period. The document details capital expenditures totaling 4.5 billion dollars, primarily allocated toward compute procurement and cloud infrastructure leasing. The offering will be the largest initial public offering in the generative AI sector to date.

In a major shift for the company's labor structure, Microsoft has initiated a voluntary buyout program for approximately sixty thousand employees. The move follows two years of strategic pivots toward enterprise AI integration and represents a reduction of roughly 25 percent of the company’s total global workforce. Eligible staff members are being offered a severance package consisting of twelve months of salary and accelerated vesting of equity grants. This restructuring replaces previous departmental operations with centralized units focused exclusively on machine learning deployment. The program is designed to reduce operational overhead by 15 billion dollars annually as the company shifts resources away from legacy consumer software divisions.

On the corporate acquisition front, Thrive Holdings, led by Joshua Kushner, is purchasing security firm ADT for 7.6 billion dollars. The all-cash deal is slated to close in the first quarter of next year, at which point ADT will transition from a public company to a private entity. The purchase price represents an 18 percent premium over ADT’s closing share price on the day prior to the announcement. Thrive Holdings intends to integrate ADT’s residential monitoring infrastructure with its existing portfolio of property-tech investments. This acquisition marks the end of ADT's second tenure as a publicly traded company following its 2018 initial public offering.

Staying in the AI sector, Deepgram has acquired Daily, a developer of real-time voice and video infrastructure. The acquisition integrates Daily's framework into Deepgram's existing voice processing stack to expand its capabilities in low-latency conversational AI. The integration aims to reduce round-trip latency for voice-based agents to under 200 milliseconds, a requirement for real-time customer service automation. Daily’s infrastructure currently supports over 500 million minutes of audio and video transmission per month. The deal consolidates the market for speech-to-text processing and real-time communication platforms, which previously operated as distinct service layers.

Also in startup news, the AI agent platform Wabi has closed a 55 million dollar funding round, pushing its total valuation to 1.25 billion dollars. The company develops tools that allow users to automate tasks through personal AI agents and autonomous mini-applications. The Series C round was led by venture capital firms specializing in autonomous systems and software-as-a-service. Wabi reports that its platform currently manages over 4 million active tasks per day, ranging from automated schedule management to complex procurement workflows. The new capital will be used to expand the firm’s engineering team and increase server capacity for its agent orchestration engine.

Finally, a massive data breach has exposed the personal records of 41,000 Department of Homeland Security employees. The leak includes sensitive details identifying undercover immigration enforcement agents, government surveillance units, and internal access credentials. The breach originated from an unsecured database managed by a third-party contractor providing payroll and benefits processing services. The exposed files contain unencrypted social security numbers, residential addresses, and historical performance evaluations. Government cybersecurity teams have confirmed that the data was accessible via a public-facing port for approximately 72 hours before being secured.
