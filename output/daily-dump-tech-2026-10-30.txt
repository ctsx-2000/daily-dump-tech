It is October 30. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

Binance has confirmed a major security breach resulting in the theft of 6.3 billion dollars in cryptocurrency. The exchange reported that hackers compromised an internal employee laptop to bypass multi-factor authentication and facilitate the unauthorized transfer of funds. Three billion dollars of the stolen assets have been identified across various decentralized finance protocols, and the company is currently working with international exchanges to freeze those specific accounts. Authorities have formally linked the attack to North Korean state-sponsored hacking groups, citing forensic evidence found in the wallet signatures that matches previous exploits attributed to the Lazarus Group. This theft represents the single largest loss of digital assets in the history of the exchange, surpassing the previous 2022 breach that cost the platform 570 million dollars.

In the infrastructure space, a startup called Runware is attempting to industrialize data center construction. The company is building a dedicated factory designed to produce prefabricated AI data centers on an assembly line, utilizing methods similar to automotive manufacturing to reduce onsite labor hours by an estimated 40 percent. The project is currently testing whether modular, mass-produced data centers can meet the high-capacity power and cooling demands of modern AI training clusters. These units are designed to house 500 kilowatts of server capacity per module, replacing traditional site-built facilities that typically require 18 to 24 months to bring online. The startup aims to shorten that deployment timeline to under six months by shipping fully integrated power distribution and thermal management systems directly to the site.

On the legal front, General Motors has agreed to a 220-million-dollar settlement following allegations that it sold customer driving data to insurance companies. The data, collected through the company's Smart Driver program, included precise telematics such as hard braking events, rapid acceleration, and speed statistics, which were shared with third-party data brokers without explicit or informed user consent. The settlement resolves claims that the automaker prioritized data monetization over customer privacy by failing to disclose how granular driving habits were being utilized to influence insurance premiums. Under the terms of the agreement, General Motors must now provide clear, separate opt-in disclosures for data sharing and delete all previously collected telematics data associated with users who did not authorize the program.

Turning to social platforms, Reddit is transitioning toward a logged-in-only experience for its users. The company reported that search engine traffic and total page views from non-authenticated visitors declined by 12 percent during the last quarter, a drop the company attributes to aggressive crawling by third-party AI scrapers. The move is designed to force visitors to create accounts and authenticate to view platform content, effectively walling off discussions from public search indexing. This change follows the company’s recent decision to charge for API access, which previously allowed researchers and developers to access public discourse without authentication. The platform now requires a verified email address to bypass the login wall that appears when a user attempts to view specific threads or community pages.

Finally, Microsoft has released a significant update for Windows 11 that includes a new cloud recovery feature for PCs that fail to boot. This utility allows users to reinstall the operating system directly from the cloud without needing external recovery media or a physical USB drive. The patch also addresses long-standing interface annoyances, including the removal of forced search bar animations and a redesigned volume mixer that allows per-app audio control. The update arrives as the company reports a 7 percent decline in overall PC revenue for the most recent fiscal period, marking the fourth consecutive quarter of slowing hardware sales. Microsoft has indicated that this update is part of a broader strategy to increase user retention on the existing Windows 11 install base, which currently accounts for approximately 30 percent of all active Windows devices.
