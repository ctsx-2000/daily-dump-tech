It is October 31. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

A malicious version of the Claude Code extension for Visual Studio Code was distributed through the Cursor marketplace for eleven hours on October 29. The compromised extension, which masqueraded as a legitimate tool for AI-assisted programming, exposed up to five thousand developers to malware specifically designed to install persistent SSH backdoors on their machines. These backdoors granted unauthorized remote access to the underlying local development environments, allowing for the exfiltration of environment variables and private configuration files. Security researchers identified the payload within the extension’s update script, which initiated a hidden connection to a remote command-and-control server immediately upon execution.

Apple is expanding its AI reach, as Apple Intelligence will launch in China with version twenty-seven point three of its operating system. A beta release is scheduled for November 4, followed by a full rollout of the features to Chinese users. This release follows regulatory hurdles regarding local data residency requirements for generative models. The update enables localized versions of writing tools, notification summaries, and image cleanup features. These capabilities replace standard system-level text processing and photo editing tools currently available in the existing Chinese release of the operating system.

On the hardware front, NVIDIA is set to launch its GeForce RTX 5080 graphics card as early as December. The new model will feature twenty-four gigabytes of VRAM, marking a significant increase over the sixteen gigabyte models currently available in the RTX 4080 series. The card utilizes a new memory architecture designed for higher bandwidth, which increases the data transfer rate by forty percent compared to the previous generation. This capacity upgrade targets high-resolution rendering and local inference tasks that previously required the more expensive RTX 4090 tier of hardware.

In enterprise software, Salesforce has announced a deal to acquire defense tech startup Rune for three hundred seventy-five million dollars. The acquisition is intended to integrate Rune’s AI logistics capabilities into the existing Missionforce defense platform, which currently manages supply chain visibility for federal contracts. Rune’s software utilizes predictive modeling to forecast inventory requirements for large-scale military operations. The integration replaces the manual data reconciliation tools previously used within the Missionforce dashboard, consolidating logistics tracking and automated replenishment scheduling into a single interface.

Intel stock experienced a sharp decline today, resulting in a twelve billion dollar loss for the government’s stake in the company. The drop follows reports that weakening PC sales are being compounded by the AI industry prioritizing memory and other hardware components, leading to higher costs for general computing devices. Revenue from the Client Computing Group fell ten percent year-over-year, while capital expenditure requirements for foundry operations remain at record levels. The current valuation reflects a reduction in market share for data center processors as competing architectures capture segments of the cloud infrastructure market.

Finally, Google is expanding its retail footprint with the opening of a new store in Houston on November 6. This marks the twelfth physical Google Store location globally and the eleventh within the United States. The location will showcase the full current hardware lineup, including the Pixel 9 series, the Pixel Watch 3, and Nest smart home equipment. This store occupies a three thousand square foot space in a major retail district, following the architectural layout established by the company’s flagship Chelsea location in New York.
