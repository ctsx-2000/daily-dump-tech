It is November 01, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

A security researcher has released a proof of concept exploit for a newly patched Android vulnerability known as the SMS of Death. Tracked as C-V-E two-thousand-twenty-six-fifty-eight-eighty-eight-two, the flaw allows an attacker to remotely crash an unpatched device or disrupt cellular service by sending a specially crafted text message. The vulnerability exists within the telephony stack of the Android Open Source Project, specifically affecting the way the system handles incoming packet headers. It impacts all devices running Android versions thirteen through sixteen, encompassing roughly two billion active handsets globally. The patch issued by Google as part of the November security bulletin modifies the memory buffer management to reject malformed data packets before they reach the system kernel.

In gaming, Phantom Blade Zero has hit a milestone on the Steam platform. Within twelve hours of its October twenty-ninth launch, the title reached over seven hundred and twenty thousand concurrent players according to Steam data. This figure surpasses the launch-day record previously held by Black Myth: Wukong for an action-RPG on the platform. The game, developed by S-Game, occupies sixty-two gigabytes of storage and utilizes the latest version of the Unreal Engine. Current player density is highest in the East Asian and North American markets, with initial server loads requiring the deployment of thirty additional regional data clusters to maintain latency below sixty milliseconds.

Regarding AI tools, Anthropic has updated its usage policies for Claude. The platform now prohibits users from using the model to create or remake video games, marking a shift in how the company is constraining its generative output. This policy change applies to all tiers, including the enterprise-grade API, and specifically targets the generation of executable game code and assets. The company stated this restriction is intended to mitigate risks associated with automated software distribution and potential copyright infringement within game development pipelines. Users who previously relied on the model for code scaffolding now face a total blockade on prompts that request game-loop architecture or interactive asset generation.

On the business front, Google is adjusting its Gemini subscription tiers. The company is removing Pro access from its lowest-priced Gemini plan, requiring users to upgrade to a higher tier to retain those specific feature sets. The entry-level plan, priced at nine dollars and ninety-nine cents, now restricts users to the Gemini Flash model, which features a smaller context window of thirty-two thousand tokens. Pro users, who pay nineteen dollars and ninety-nine cents, retain access to the one million token context window and the ability to execute complex reasoning tasks. This transition affects approximately forty million subscribers who were previously granted temporary access to Pro-level features as part of a promotional window that expired yesterday.

Apple leadership has addressed the company’s current approach to artificial intelligence. CEO Tim Cook stated that the organization is not pursuing AI for its own sake, emphasizing a focus on specific product integration rather than broad, feature-heavy releases. The company is currently allocating seventy percent of its research and development budget toward on-device silicon optimization rather than cloud-based large language model training. This strategy shifts the focus away from the industry-wide trend of increasing parameter counts, prioritizing instead the thermal efficiency and battery life of the upcoming M-series chipsets. Apple’s current internal metrics show a four-fold increase in neural engine operations per watt compared to the previous fiscal year.

In infrastructure news, the supersonic airliner startup Boom is pivoting its resources. The company is shifting focus toward powering AI data centers, which has resulted in delays for its aircraft development and the loss of a major client in its data center power division. The Overture aircraft project, which was intended to transport passengers at Mach one-point-seven, has been pushed back by three years as engineering staff are reassigned to modular power grid projects. This pivot comes after the company failed to meet the milestone for its first flight test, originally scheduled for December. The loss of the client, a major hyperscale cloud provider, leaves a shortfall of four hundred million dollars in projected research funding for the next eighteen months.

Finally, the provost of Dartmouth College has been removed from his position following revelations regarding his own use of AI. The official had previously issued warnings to the campus community about the risks of AI-assisted cheating before it was discovered that he had utilized the technology in his own professional work. An internal audit found that three major administrative reports and two faculty-wide addresses authored by the provost were generated using a commercial chatbot without proper attribution. The college’s board of trustees confirmed that the provost’s actions violated the institution's updated academic integrity policy, which mandates the disclosure of all generative tools used in official administrative communications.
