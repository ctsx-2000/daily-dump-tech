It is November 4, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

Revolut has reached a valuation of 115 billion dollars following a new share sale. This figure represents a 53 percent increase over the company’s previous valuation of 45 billion dollars established in 2024. The fintech company, which is preparing to launch a full banking service in the United States, secured this capital through a secondary share sale led by investment firms Coatue and T. Rowe Price. The transaction allows existing employees and early investors to liquidate equity while providing the company with additional capital reserves to meet regulatory capital requirements for its pending U.S. banking license.

Anthropic is developing a system called FlashProve designed to verify the origin of AI-generated content. The tool functions as a cryptographic signing mechanism integrated directly into the model’s inference pipeline, replacing manual watermarking methods that were previously susceptible to removal via data compression or image editing. FlashProve allows users to confirm that a response originated from a verified Anthropic model rather than a third-party clone or a spoofed endpoint, ensuring the integrity of the data stream. By validating the output against the model’s unique public key, the system provides a persistent audit trail for enterprises handling sensitive or automated documentation.

The Bluetooth Special Interest Group has officially adopted High Data Throughput as an optional feature within the Bluetooth Core 7.0 specification. This update increases the maximum sustained data transfer speed for Bluetooth Low Energy devices to 8 megabits per second, a significant jump from the 2 megabits per second limit maintained since the Bluetooth 5.0 release. The feature utilizes a wider channel bandwidth to reduce latency for high-fidelity audio streaming and real-time data synchronization between wearables and mobile handsets. This protocol change replaces the existing modulation scheme with a more efficient physical layer, effectively quadrupling the available bandwidth for peripheral communication.

Uber executives disclosed in their latest earnings call that their platform facilitated only four rides arranged by third-party AI agents during the third quarter of this year. While AI-driven orders for Uber Eats accounted for approximately 15 percent of total food delivery volume, the data confirms that autonomous agent-to-agent booking for transport remains at a negligible scale. The company reported 2.9 billion total trips for the quarter, meaning agent-initiated transport requests represent less than 0.000001 percent of total ride-hailing activity. Current infrastructure for third-party AI integration is limited by API rate-limiting and the lack of standardized protocols for non-human user authentication.

Large-scale compute infrastructure is facing new headwinds as voters across the United States recently approved ballot measures and elected local officials favoring moratoriums on data center construction. In states including Virginia and Arizona, new zoning ordinances prohibit the development of facilities exceeding 50 megawatts of power consumption within proximity to residential water grids. These regulatory obstacles are slowing expansion plans for hyperscalers relying on high server density, creating a measurable friction point for the industry’s physical footprint. The restrictions are forcing companies to relocate planned server farms to remote regions with lower grid integration, increasing the cost of latency-sensitive compute operations.

On the hardware front, Sony has removed the PlayStation VR 2 headset from its official web store as stock disappears from third-party retailers. The move signals the conclusion of production for the peripheral, which served as the primary virtual reality hardware for the PlayStation 5 ecosystem since its February 2023 launch. Third-party electronics retailers now report that inventory is restricted to remaining open-box units, with no scheduled manufacturing runs to replenish supply channels. The discontinuation coincides with a shift in Sony’s hardware strategy toward prioritizing software development for its core console over dedicated VR hardware peripherals.

Finally, NVIDIA is preparing for a price increase on its GeForce RTX 50-series gaming graphics cards. Reports from supply chain partners indicate that the Blackwell-based cards will see price adjustments of up to 500 dollars effective this week. The increase applies to the flagship 5090 and 5080 models, bringing their suggested retail prices to 2,499 dollars and 1,699 dollars respectively. This revision is driven by sustained high demand for the new GPU architecture and ongoing capacity constraints at semiconductor fabrication facilities, which have limited the volume of available units since the product line's initial release.
