Meta has released Muse Ember, a new lightweight model designed for agent execution. It is priced at ten cents per million input tokens, positioning it as a lower-cost alternative to existing frontier models like Haiku and Luna. The model utilizes a distilled architecture specifically trained on tool-use datasets, which Meta reports reduces latency by forty-two percent compared to its predecessor, Llama 4-Lite. This release targets high-volume automated tasks, replacing general-purpose models that previously incurred costs of thirty cents per million tokens. The inference efficiency allows for deployment on edge devices with as little as four gigabytes of available system memory.

In other AI business, Anthropic has committed three point four billion dollars to purchase natural gas turbines. The deal marks a significant move by the AI company to secure dedicated power infrastructure for its data centers. These turbines are rated for a combined capacity of two gigawatts, which is sufficient to power approximately one point five million homes. This acquisition replaces reliance on standard grid-purchasing agreements, providing the company with direct control over its energy supply chain. The infrastructure will be distributed across three primary data centers in the Midwest, specifically chosen for their proximity to existing natural gas pipelines.

Turning to autonomous driving, federal regulators report that Waymo robotaxis have continued to illegally pass school buses. This behavior persisted even after the company rolled out a software update intended to correct the issue nearly one year ago. Data from the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration indicates that sixty-four incidents have been recorded in the past six months, occurring primarily in urban zones where the vehicles operate at speeds below twenty-five miles per hour. The software update failed to account for flashing red lights on school buses when the vehicles were executing right-hand turns at intersections. Investigators found that the sensor suite correctly identified the bus type but prioritized local traffic flow rules over the specific state-level stop requirements.

Samsung is testing a new subscription service called Galaxy AI Premium. The internal app data indicates a paid tier that offers additional AI credits, advanced video generation features for photos, and expanded automation capabilities for the Bixby assistant. This service structure replaces the previous free access model for cloud-based generative tasks. Users will be capped at five hundred credits per month under the base plan, with the premium tier offering unlimited generation. The integration of Bixby automation allows for the creation of multi-step workflows that can span across third-party smart home applications.

On the hardware front, an investigation into LG display panels has identified a defect known as edge rot. Testing shows this pixel degradation affects eighty-seven point five percent of older WOLED screens, and while the manufacturer has acknowledged the issue, newer panel generations are still under review. The defect manifests as a discoloration along the perimeter of the screen, caused by the oxidation of the organic light-emitting diode material when exposed to atmospheric moisture. This failure typically appears after three thousand hours of operation. The affected units span the two thousand twenty-three and twenty-four product lines, impacting approximately four million individual display panels currently in use globally.

Finally, Uber has integrated parking reservations directly into its mobile application. The feature is the result of the company's recent acquisition of SpotHero, providing users with access to booking and payment tools for over thirteen thousand parking facilities across the country. The integration removes the requirement for users to toggle between the ride-hailing app and separate parking management interfaces. Reservations are synced in real-time with the vehicle’s location data, allowing the application to adjust the final drop-off point to the specific parking stall reserved by the user. The system supports dynamic pricing based on hourly demand and proximity to major event venues.
