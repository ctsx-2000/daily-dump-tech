It is November 12, 2026. This is your Daily Dump of tech news.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has resigned from his position amid reports of multiple personal affairs, including one involving a woman suspected of spying for the Chinese government. The board of directors confirmed the departure early this morning, appointing former Chief Operating Officer Colette Kress as interim chief executive. Huang has agreed to testify before the House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party this Wednesday following threats of a formal subpoena. The inquiry centers on whether proprietary hardware specifications or non-public strategic roadmaps were disclosed during these personal interactions.

In security news, ConnectWise is investigating an active malware campaign targeting managed service providers. Attackers are exploiting compromised ScreenConnect environments to deploy rogue remote-access clients onto customer devices across multiple organizations. The campaign utilizes stolen administrative credentials to bypass multi-factor authentication, allowing for persistent access to downstream client networks. Forensic reports indicate that the attackers are pivoting from these remote management portals to install ransomware payloads and exfiltrate sensitive backup data.

Turning to the airline industry, United Airlines has implemented a ban on the use of AI agents for booking flights or managing reservations. The company cites significant cybersecurity risks and potential data integrity issues associated with automated third-party booking tools that utilize screen-scraping techniques to interface with legacy reservation systems. This policy applies to all third-party automated programs that bypass official application programming interfaces. United reports that these unauthorized tools have caused systematic errors in passenger name records and triggered automated cancellations for high-frequency travelers.

Related to AI risk, a new study indicates that major AI chatbots, including ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity, are frequently recommending fake or high-risk retail websites to users. Researchers found that when prompted to find products or retailers, these models directed shoppers toward fraudulent sites designed to capture personal and financial information. Analysis revealed that these recommendations often featured non-existent storefronts that mimic legitimate brands, with 40 percent of the identified sites lacking secure payment encryption. The models consistently prioritized these high-risk sites in search results despite the presence of documented phishing warnings associated with those domains.

On the hardware front, Sony has officially announced that the PlayStation 5 has surpassed 100 million units sold. The console achieved this milestone 72 months after its initial launch in November 2020, reaching the mark approximately 18 months faster than its predecessor, the PlayStation 4. Financial disclosures show that hardware revenue remains the primary driver of Sony’s gaming division, accounting for 62 percent of total quarterly earnings. The milestone comes as Sony prepares to transition focus toward mid-cycle hardware refreshes and expanded cloud-based gaming services.

Finally, New York regulators have penalized T-Mobile for illegally imposing a 50 gigabyte data cap on its low-income home internet plan. The state mandates a minimum service level of 25 megabits per second for subsidized home broadband, but investigators found that T-Mobile throttled user speeds to 600 kilobits per second once the data limit was reached. The New York Public Service Commission issued a 15 million dollar fine, noting that the throttling policy violated the terms of the service agreement established during the 2024 state broadband expansion initiative. T-Mobile is now required to provide retroactive credits to the 45,000 households affected by the service degradation.
